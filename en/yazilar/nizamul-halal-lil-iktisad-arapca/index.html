The Social, Political and Strategic Potential of the Halal Economic System

January 2, 2009About 31 min read

Translated from the Arabic original.Read the original

This English text was translated from the Turkish version of the same paper.

I. Definitions and Terms

The halal economic system has fundamental principles of its own. This system is also called the economic order of Islam. Islam, the only religion sent to humanity by Almighty Allah through the prophets, has laid down principles suited to the nature and character of individuals and societies, and has set out rulings on every field of economics.

Iktisad (economy), in its dictionary sense, means "that which lies between the praised and the blamed, between justice and oppression, that which is between the near and the far",(1) keeping to the middle way without falling into either excess or neglect,(2) and "saving, thrift, being moderate".(3) The word "qasd/qist", which is its root, expresses the meaning of "being just, being fair, the road being perfectly straight, being on the right course".(4) Qistas, which comes from the same root, means measure, standard. Thus we may say that it is "to spend where spending is due, neither going to waste nor falling into miserliness". It is not to depart from justice and measure in production, distribution and consumption.

Iktisad as a term: "It is the use of the resources of production by people in order to produce various goods and to share these goods, today or at a future time, among the groups or individuals that make up the community."(5) Or: "It is the branch of knowledge that studies, from various sides, the subject of production, consumption and goods."(6) In this sense the science of iktisad is called the science of economics.

"Economical" means "more convenient, more suitable, more profitable".

Halal, for its part, is an Arabic word meaning to untie a knot; figuratively it is everything that is "permitted, allowed, not forbidden, free and lawful".(7) It is the one who forbids untying the ban and setting the thing free, taking it out of the forbidden, removing the ban.(8)

As a term it means "the acts that Allah and His Messenger have not forbidden and have not found ugly, and that one is free to do".(9)

In the Noble Qur'an: "Today the good and clean things have been made lawful for you. The food of those who were given the Book is lawful for you, and your food is lawful for them."(10)

"Eat of what is lawful and good among the things you have gained, and be mindful of Allah. Surely Allah is most forgiving, most merciful."(11)

"O you who believe! Do not forbid the clean things that Allah has made lawful for you, and do not overstep the limit. Surely Allah does not love those who overstep the bounds. Eat of what is lawful and good among the things Allah has given you as provision. Be mindful of Allah, in whom you believe."(12)

In the noble hadiths of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) it is expressed as follows: "(To take) the property of a Muslim without his heartfelt consent is not lawful."(13)

According to what is narrated from Nu'man ibn Bashir (may Allah be pleased with him), the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "The lawful is perfectly clear and the forbidden is perfectly clear. Between the two there are doubtful things which most people do not know. Whoever guards himself against the doubtful things has protected his religion and his honour. And whoever turns towards these doubtful things may fall into what is forbidden and prohibited. Just so, a shepherd who grazes his animals around a reserved pasture with boundaries is very likely to cross into it. Take heed! Every king has a reserved pasture. Take heed! The reserved pasture of Allah is His prohibitions. Take heed! In the body there is a piece of flesh: if it is sound, the whole body is sound; if it is corrupted, the whole body is corrupted. That is the heart."(14)

As is seen in the verses and hadiths, the concept of halal is used as the opposite of haram.

Haram is "an act which the Book and the authentic Sunnah require, in a definite and binding manner, not to be done".(15)

Makruh is the conduct that is better left undone than done.

Mashbuh means the things that are doubtful, on which there is no definite and firm ruling.

II. The Principles of the Halal Economic System

The Halal Economic System is the one and only alternative to the capitalist economic system of the Western bloc, to the Marxist/socialist economic system of the Eastern bloc, and to all man-made systems. It has set out its own principles independently, far from the mystical outlook of Christianity that draws people away from the life of this world, from philosophies that exalt only the spirit as in Asian religions such as Buddhism, Manichaeism and Brahmanism, and from the ethnic foundation of Judaism.

We may list these principles as follows:

a- The halal economic system is, before all else, divine.

The principles of this system have been laid down by Allah, who created human beings, the earth and the heavens and the universe out of nothing. All the prophets are those who applied this system and its exemplary models. The divine books are the written documents that determine its laws and are taken as reference.

b- The halal economic system believes in the hereafter; it is not worldly/secular.

Every individual, every society and every government that represents society is responsible for all that it does. This responsibility will exist in the hereafter as it does in this world; everyone, every institution and every administration will be called to account for what it has done. Because it holds belief in the hereafter, it does not set matter and worldly goods upon its head as a crown; it looks upon them as a trust. Matter and meaning go forward together.

Ibn Khaldun expresses this as follows: "The Lawgiver (the one who lays down the religion/law: Allah) regards the whole world and its wealth, every state and condition, as a means, a step towards the hereafter. Where the means is lacking, it is impossible to reach the goal…"(16)

c- This system is a model that works on the basis of mutual risk.(17)

The Islamic law of partnerships rests on the principle of mutual risk. There is no question of the absolute profit of one side or the absolute loss of the other. In all commercial and economic affairs it takes the principle of profit and loss into account. For this reason, commercial activities based on one-sided risk have been forbidden.

The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) forbade the sale in advance of the crop of a field or an orchard before ripeness has yet appeared.

Likewise, he did not approve of, and forbade, selling to someone else goods that have not been taken into possession once the contract of sale has been concluded.(18) For a customer to sell any foodstuff he has bought to someone else before taking delivery of it and moving it amounts to selling money for money. In this way the customer's goods are deferred. Selling the fish in the sea and the bird in the air is also of this kind.

d- In this system, eating, drinking and using halal products has been commanded, and all haram products have been forbidden.

Halal covers the products that religion approves of, that are clean, pleasant, harmless and wholesome. Using halal products and turning towards them has been counted as worship.

Haram, for its part, covers the products that religion does not approve of, that are filthy, that appear ugly to human nature, that are harmful and unwholesome. In an Islamic society, eating, drinking, using, selling and buying all of these, in short their production and consumption, has been forbidden.

Makruh and mashbuh products have been counted as doubtful, and every Muslim has been advised to keep away from them. Keeping away from doubtful things has been described as taqwa (God-consciousness).

e- Zakat, which secures social solidarity and balance, has been made obligatory.

Zakat is a principle of solidarity and mutual help, taken from the rich and distributed to the poor.

Almighty Allah has said on this subject:

"Seek the abode of the hereafter by spending out of the wealth that Allah has given you."(19)

"They (those believers) are such that, if We give them a standing on the earth, they perform the prayer properly, give the zakat, enjoin what is good and strive to turn people away from evil."(20)

"The alms, as an obligation from Allah, are only for the poor, the needy, those employed in the work (of zakat), those whose hearts are to be won over, slaves, debtors, in the way of Allah and for those stranded on the road. Allah is all-knowing, possessor of judgment and wisdom."(21)

Allah has threatened those who do not secure social solidarity, who do not meet the needs of the poor, the debtors, the slaves and the destitute, and who gather wealth in certain hands, and has declared them deserving of His severe punishment.

"Give tidings of a painful punishment to those who hoard gold and silver and do not spend them in the cause of Allah!"(22)

Thanks to zakat, the gulf between rich and poor has been removed, poverty and misery have been eased, and the level of people of low income has been raised. An atmosphere in which the poor would hate the owners of wealth and become their enemies, and the rich would look on the poor with distaste, has been done away with.

f- Every individual in society has been insured.

In an Islamic society every individual has been insured, whether Muslim or non-Muslim, whether innocent or guilty.

Every individual's unemployment insurance, health insurance and social rights (the basic necessities: food, drink, education, marriage, shelter and the others) have been guaranteed by society. The greatest duty of society is to help the individual in all his efforts, to guide him and to make his affairs easier. In this way the greatest support for the individual in winning happiness in this world and in the hereafter is provided and made real.

g- The halal economy builds empathy with society and the individuals of society.

Giving short measure and short weight has been forbidden, and honesty has been made the basis of buying and selling and of all commercial activities.

To deceive has been counted as haram, and to let oneself knowingly be deceived as a sin. In the Market of Medina that he himself established, the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) ordered a seller who was hiding the wetness of his produce not to do so, saying "Whoever deceives us is not one of us",(23) and made clear that deceivers move away from the line of Islam. With the saying "The believer is not bitten twice from the snake's hole",(24) it was not looked on kindly that consumers should be deceived either. He also commanded truthfulness in all commercial activities and gave the news that "the honest merchant will be gathered together with the prophets and the martyrs".(25)

In a society where the producer does not deceive and the consumer is not deceived, empathy is naturally realised at the highest level. In this way the wheels of the economy turn and work soundly and regularly, without breaking or bending.

h- Relations between worker and employer have been set on the foundation of goodness.

Labour has a very great importance in the progress and development of Islamic civilisation. For this reason it does not look down on labour or belittle it; yet neither does it place labour in a sacred position.

With the words "Seek your provision with Allah",(26) it has been stated that working is a command of Allah. Thus working and striving has been counted as worship, while laziness has been forbidden.

A person's manual labour/producing has been counted as the most acceptable earning. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him): "The most lawful of what a servant eats is the earning from the manual labour of the craftsman."(27) And again: "No one has ever eaten a better provision than that from his own earning."(28)

Employers have been commanded to protect the rights of the one who works, and those who do the opposite have been punished. The one who works has likewise been forbidden to harm the place and the business where he works, and such conduct has been regarded as betrayal.

By saying "Give the worker his wage before his sweat has dried",(29) the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) prevented the worker's labour from being wasted.

"Being a burden on people"(30) has not been looked on kindly, and the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), saying "The best of you are those who are not a burden on people's backs",(31) declared that sitting idle, not working and so becoming a burden on other people is not good conduct.

i- In the halal economy individual ownership has been approved.

There is no question either of individual ownership alone, as in capitalism, or of the ownership of society only, as in socialism.

To a great extent it has made the ownership of land too particular to the individual, and has left the working of the land and farming to the individual as well.

"The lands that are taken from the oppressive group and pass into the ownership of society are represented by the caliph/government so as to be put at the service of the people."(32)

This system, which both gives the individual the right of ownership and approves of society being an owner, has always succeeded in striking a balance between the two.

k- Lending has been made a matter for the state.

Those who take and give interest argue that interest is necessary and indispensable for society, saying that if interest is abolished no lender will be found, and asking who will give credit.

In the halal economy, lending has been accepted as the business of the government. The government, acting with the understanding of a social state, provides credit to those in need and in cases of necessity, and asks no fee and no interest in return for these services. For a part of the zakat revenues has been set aside to be given to debtors and to clear their debts. In this way an effort is made to lessen, or to remove altogether, the misery and unhappiness of the poor and the needy.

l- The national wealth has been shared out continually, without ever stopping, and kept in flow.(33)

Thanks to the law of inheritance and bequest applied by Islam, the piling up and gathering of wealth in the hands of a handful of people has been prevented. Inheritance has been divided and the rulings on bequests carried out, and so this flow has been realised.

m- In this system, taking and giving riba/interest has been forbidden.

Islam has forbidden the taking of riba/interest on money given as a loan. It has thus prevented the poor from becoming poorer still and being crushed under the wheels of exploitation, a gulf from opening between rich and poor, and wealth from becoming a monopoly.

Riba/interest is the greatest obstacle to a just sharing of income. It causes riches/wealth to gather in certain hands, injustice/wrong to prevail, markets to shrink, and the people who form the majority to fall into poverty.

Because of these causes Almighty Allah has made interest haram and buying and selling halal:

"(We did so) because of their taking riba/interest though they had been forbidden it, and their devouring people's wealth unjustly. For the unbelievers among them We have prepared a most painful punishment."(34)

"Those who devour riba/interest do not rise except as one rises whom Satan has struck down. That is because they say: 'Buying and selling is only like riba/interest.' Yet Allah has made trade lawful and interest forbidden…"(35)

And the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him):

"He cursed the one who devours interest, the one who gives it, the one who writes it down and the one who witnesses it; all of them are equal in the offence."(36)

Interest at the same time prevents risk from being shared between the parties. In commercial and industrial enterprises the party in hardship and difficulty has borne the risk, while the party giving the interest-bearing loan has taken no risk at all.

Interest, the football pools, lotto, the national lottery and other games of chance, which are gambling, and the stock market shares that are traded on the market almost like gambling, present the appearance of a financial value that does not exist, greater than the real values. The financial crisis of the capitalist and liberal world that began in September 2008 shows us this truth. This is not a global crisis but the structural crisis of capitalism. More precisely, it is the collapse of capitalism.

Ömer Cihad Vardan, President of the Independent Industrialists' and Businessmen's Association of Turkey (MÜSİAD), states that while the real economy today amounts to 50 trillion $, the derivatives of finance amount to 175 trillion $. There is an excess of exactly 3.5 times over the real values that exist, which appears as unjust gain.(37) Thus the capitalist economy has been built on virtual values that do not exist in reality, and it has ended in fiasco.

n- Ihtikar/hoarding has been forbidden.

Ihtikar is to hold back and not sell the goods that the people need, while watching for high prices. It makes no difference whether the goods the people need are of the kind of food and drink (provision) or goods other than these.

On this subject the following hadiths have been narrated from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him):

"The one who hoards is surely disobedient, a sinner."(38)

"The merchant is given provision, while the hoarder is cursed."(39)

"Whoever hoards a foodstuff for forty days, watching for a high price, moves away from Allah, and Allah moves away from him."(40)

"What a bad servant the hoarder is! If Allah lowers prices he is put out and grieved, and if He raises them he rejoices."(41)

In this situation the halal economy intervenes in the market and punishes the hoarders. Unlike in the free market economy, not everyone has the right to do whatever he wishes.

o- Waste and squandering have been forbidden.

Israf (waste) is to consume more than is needed, to spend property in vain and where there is no need, and to destroy it. It is to go beyond moderation and abandon measure. It is to go to excess in committing sin and so to fall into injustice and oppression.

In the Noble Qur'an it mostly comes in the form "musrifun: those who waste". Elsewhere it is expressed as follows:

"Do not waste. Surely He does not love those who waste."(42)

"Eat and drink, and do not waste. Surely He does not love those who waste."(43)

It has been declared that those who waste and squander, living in luxury and pomp, are the brothers of Satan. Thus these people have taken themselves off the right way and dragged themselves into a channel that follows the track of Satan.

"Surely the squanderers have become the brothers of the satans. And Satan is ungrateful to his Lord."(44)

The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) also said on this subject:
"Allah makes rich the one who is thrifty."(45) And the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said three times: "Hold fast to thrift."(46)

III. The social potential of the halal economy

The basic rules that Islam has laid down for economic life are in every respect applicable in society, realistic and far from fantasy; they secure solidarity among individuals, prevent grudge, hatred, anger and enmity towards one another, protect the social balance, keep away from excess and rest on the principle of measure.

First of all, by taking empathy as the basis of economic relations, it strongly protects the social fabric. It brings this principle to life through many rules:

To give charity so secretly that the left hand cannot hear what the right hand gives,

To prefer one's brother to one's own self,

Not to cast up to people what one has spent on them,

To declare that a good deed followed by hurt and reproach comes to nothing,

To accept zakat as the right of the poor and the needy,

Not to scold the one who asks for something and begs,

To feed the poor,

To stretch out a hand of compassion to the orphan and not to oppress him,

To teach not to go to bed full while one's neighbour is hungry,

To count as worship the good done to one's wife, children, relatives, neighbours and all people,

Not to deceive when selling and producing,

To take care not to be deceived when buying and consuming,

Never to lie,

To carry out the distribution and sharing of income justly,

To pay the wage of the person one employs before his sweat has dried,

To treat well the people one employs,

To insure the unemployed from the state budget at a level that meets all their basic needs,

To pray in the same row together with one's worker,

And so to do away with class distinction,

Not to cheat or give short in measure and weight,

Not to seek goods for which one's brother is already the customer,

Not to swear much by Allah when selling,

Not to look down on people and on the working class,

Not to be the servant and slave of capital,

To set forth the truth that the property and wealth given to a person is a trust,

Not to run after worldly ambitions,

To cry out to all humanity the truth that superiority lies not in capital, beauty, lineage, office or rank, and that true superiority lies in taqwa,

And to forbid a life and a consumption of luxury, pomp and waste.

All these rules raise empathy in society to its peak, secure the trust of individuals in one another, increase tranquillity, bring an atmosphere of peace and make these good things lasting.

By commanding the eating, drinking and use of halal products, and by forbidding those that are haram, the corruption of the structure of society has been prevented. Through the production and consumption of harmless, clean and wholesome products, first the individual and then the foundations of the social structure have been protected, and the raising of healthy and vigorous generations has been secured. The prohibition of intoxicants and narcotics, which destroy the functions of the mind that sets the human being apart from all other beings and gives him a superior position, is so that he may not lose his human faculties and may keep his honour and dignity.

By making zakat obligatory, the sharing of wealth has been secured by taking from the rich/the one who has and giving to the poor, the gulf between rich and poor has been removed, and in this way an effort has been made to establish and protect the social balance.

By forbidding the taking and giving of interest, wealth has been prevented from passing into the hands of a handful of people and from becoming a commodity that circulates among the rich, and the exploitation and impoverishment of society has been forestalled.

By forbidding a life of waste, squandering, luxury and splendour, the upsetting of the social balance to the detriment of the poor has been prevented. Through this rule the poor, too, have been kept from seeing as enemies the rich who live in luxury and dissipation, and from looking on them with grudge and anger.

By making the application of the law of inheritance and bequest obligatory, the flow of wealth/riches to every layer of society has been secured, and its piling up in the hands of a handful of people has been prevented.

By insuring every individual, and by lending money through the state to those in need, to investors and to commercial entrepreneurs, an effort has been made to protect the balance in society, and production and the economy have been kept lively and working. In this way every effort has been made that no hungry, thirsty or unemployed people should remain in society.

In the end, by protecting the social balance the increase of crime has been prevented, and a barrier has been set against injustices, thefts, snatchings, usurpations, robberies, bribery and unjust gain. All of this gives rise, in the social sense and across a wide range, to the possibility of the halal economy being applied in every region and society.

IV. The political potential of the halal economy

A legitimate political government, which is the representative of society, is responsible in the first degree for seeing that the economic wheel turns justly and is properly ordered.

The collection of revenues: zakat, tithe (ushr), land tax (kharaj) and taxes,

The distribution of expenditures to the places where they are due,

The inspection of the bazaar and the market and, when necessary, intervention in them,

The development of policies of farming, agriculture and industry based on the principle of producing halal products and forbidding haram products,

Securing the balance of income and expenditure in the budget,

Bringing about the just sharing of revenues,

Making arrangements that will meet the basic needs of the poor and the needy,

And so keeping alive the reality of the social state: these are under the responsibility of the political government.

a- The first duty of the political government is just administration.

Umar (may Allah be pleased with him), the second Caliph of the Muslims after the Messenger of Allah, expressed this in a very concise saying: "Justice is the foundation of rule." What keeps society standing, makes it lively and dynamic and secures tranquillity and peace is justice, is adherence to law. Administrations that are not bound by law and do not take the supremacy of law as their basis destroy justice and drag societies into misery, insecurity, unrest and chaos.

b- The Collection of State Revenues

In an Islamic society there are many sources of revenue. Voluntary spending, alms/charitable gifts, zakat, taxes of financial liability, tithe, land tax, the fifth (khums), taxes on mines, spoils of war, animal products, accumulated gold and silver and other revenues make up the state budget.

The most important source of revenue of the Bayt al-Mal/the budget is zakat. Zakat is the most important element in the budget that secures social balance, solidarity and the just distribution of income. For this reason those who do not give zakat are not excused. Even if it be by war, zakat, which is the right of the poor, is taken from them and distributed to the places where it is due. For zakat secures the just distribution of income. That is why those who do not give zakat are those who destroy justice and overturn the balance in society.

c- State expenditures

The Noble Qur'an is the sole book that tells where and how the spending of the Bayt al-Mal and of the state is to be made.

How the zakat revenues are to be distributed is told by the following noble verse:(47)

  1. The poor,

  2. The needy,

  3. The persons and institutions charged with this work,

  4. Those whose hearts are to be won,

  5. Those who are to be freed from the yoke,

  6. Those sunk in heavy debts,

  7. In the way of Allah,

  8. Those who fall into hardship during a journey.

When examined closely, it is seen that these eight classes embrace almost the whole of society and include all its layers.

After the Book come the authentic Sunnah practices of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) and, as a fine legacy, the practices of the Rightly Guided Caliphs. The economic practices of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) during his ten years of governing the state are the most important examples that Muslims need to follow. The Rightly Guided Caliphs are to be regarded in the same way.

In the end, the political government carries out these arrangements and this distribution justly, and sets up every kind of mechanism and institution. It leaves no room for failures, shortcomings and errors in this matter. Officials who act wrongly, if they persist in their errors, are removed from their posts and punished.

d- Supervision by the political government

The political government supervises the economy, the bazaar and the market on behalf of the nation. It prevents conduct that departs from honesty and works against society, such as deceit, fraud, harmful production, trickery, short weight and short measure. To achieve these aims it sets up mechanisms of supervision and runs them soundly. So that these operations may run soundly and perfectly, it issues laws, statutes, circulars and regulations.

The hisba organisations formed in Islamic history, the ihtisab and the muhtasib, and in later periods the şehremaneti/the municipalities, are social institutions founded to carry out the work of supervision. These organisations, by inspecting on the spot in the bazaar and the market, prevent fraud and intrigue. Like the commercial courts of today, they settle on the spot the commercial disputes that arise among tradesmen and merchants, customers and sellers.

These organisations also deal with the customs procedures at the borders, with customs duties, fees and dues.

e- Crisis management by the political government:

In times of social disaster such as earthquake, flood, drought and famine, the political government may apply different measures according to need.

If the budget is not strong enough to meet the losses arising from these disasters, then it may impose taxes at rates that may reach the greater part of the wealth of the rich, leaving them an amount sufficient for their livelihood. These taxes that are collected are spent on the places where society is seen to have need and on needy individuals.(48)

V. The strategic potential of the halal economy

The Islamic world has strategic importance in terms of the density of its population, in terms of its human, underground and surface resources, in terms of the international organisations it has formed and in geopolitical terms. In addition, the fact that in many countries Muslims have the status of a minority of considerable size increases its strategic importance.

a- Density of population:

Of the 6 billion people in the world, more than 1.5 billion are the Muslim population. This shows that one in every four people living on earth is a Muslim. With this density of population, Islam holds the position of the second religion in the world after Christianity.

To state it in figures: the distribution of the Muslim population, which has reached 1.8 billion with an increase of around 250 per cent in the last fifty years and which, taking the present annual rate of increase of 2.9% as the basis, is estimated to double within 24 years and reach 3.6 billion people, is as follows:

• Asia (including Turkey, 72 million): 1,240 million

• Africa: 490 million

• Europe (including Russia, 27 million): 52 million

• North America: 7 million

• South America: 3 million

• Oceania: 1 million

• In total 1,793 million Muslims live in the world.

This strategic potential gained through the density of population further increases, and makes possible, the applicability of the halal economy.

b- Human resources

In terms of human resources, the Islamic world has to a great extent a young population. This forms a very large labour force and the military force that will keep it standing.

c- Underground and surface resources:

The Islamic world also possesses very great riches in terms of underground resources. The great majority of gold, diamonds, boron, oil, natural gas, iron and other minerals are found in Muslim countries. Above ground, too, in the field of agriculture and farming, it carries natural riches in its bosom.

d- Geographical and geopolitical position:

The Islamic world has geographical and geopolitical importance by virtue of the lands it possesses.

e- International organisations:

The Islamic countries have founded many international organisations and are members of many others.

We may list these organisations as follows:

1- The Organisation of the Islamic Conference (OIC). This organisation, made up of 57 Islamic countries, also has economic, cultural, social and scientific bodies. As an economic body, COMCEC (İSEDAK) holds an important place.

2- The Arab League (League of Arab States). This organisation, made up of 22 Muslim Arab states, also has economic and social bodies.

3- The D-8 Group: This independent body, founded in 1997 under the leadership of Turkey, is made up of 8 Islamic countries: Turkey, Iran, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Malaysia, Indonesia, Egypt and Nigeria.

4- The Black Sea Economic Cooperation and Development Organisation: It is an economic organisation founded, under the leadership of Turkey, mostly by Muslim Turkic and Caucasian countries.

5- The African Union. All the countries of the African continent except Morocco are members of this organisation, which was founded in 1963 for the purpose of cooperation and solidarity among the African countries. All the Islamic countries on the African continent other than Morocco are members of this union.

6- The United Nations (UN). Almost all the Islamic countries are members of the UN. This shows that a quarter of the members of the UN are Muslim countries.

7- The G-20. These countries hold a decisive political, economic and strategic position in the world. Three of the member countries are Muslim countries: Turkey, Indonesia and Saudi Arabia.

Besides these, they are the founders or members of many more international organisations, large and small, that are effective politically and strategically in their regions, such as the Islamic Development Bank. This too makes the application of the halal economy possible, both in the Islamic countries and on a world scale.
