Introduction

The cases of divorce that have been rising at a frightening rate in our country in recent years, and the fact that this number has reached about one million in ten years, are a sign that the matter has become a social wound and a problem.

Moreover, according to the statistics, negative events such as the increase of divorces and the decrease of marriages, the rise of violence within the family, the murders committed after divorces, especially against women, divorced families becoming enemies of one another, the hatred and anger between them not dying down, the children left in the middle being deprived of the love of either the mother or the father, and one of the spouses presenting the mother or the father as dead although they are alive, are rapidly speeding up the dissolution and collapse of the family, which is counted as the most basic building block of society.

We truly need to find sound and lasting solutions to this social problem that exists. If these solutions are not produced and put into practice, very destructive disasters that are hard to repair await society in the future.

These negative pictures bring to mind the question of how society came, or was brought, to this stage. Of course there are many answers to be given to this question. But one of the most important answers is that laws which do not suit the structure, the belief, the customs and the traditions of society have been applied for years.

That the Swiss civil law,2 which was inspired by Catholic Christianity and pagan Roman law, was translated into Turkish exactly as it was, and with a faulty translation at that, and has been put into force and applied from 1926 until today, has been a practice contrary to the true norms of law. For laws, in their essence, are everywhere arranged and shaped according to the demands, the preferences, the structure and the belief of societies.

The fact that 99 % of the communities living in Turkey, Turks, Kurds, Arabs, Georgians, Circassians, Laz, Abkhaz, Albanians, Bosniaks, Turkmens and others, are Muslim, and that the religion of Islam forms the cornerstone of this mosaic, indicates that we must think again about these practices in force and correct the mistakes that have been made. Indeed these negative practices, which do not conform to the norms of law, have left deep marks in society and have turned the problems into gangrene.

How happy we shall be if we too, with this very short study of ours, can be a small lancet to the problem!

In the general sense, arbitration has been an indispensable institution applied throughout history to resolve the disputes that exist in societies.

The question of "family arbitration" or the "family council", which has been applied throughout the history of Islam, will help to lighten or reduce the problem if it is applied today. We must state that this matter was made into law in the last periods of the Ottoman State and is applied in many Islamic countries today as well.

In the general sense, arbitration has been an indispensable institution applied throughout history to resolve the disputes that exist in societies. For example the last prophet, Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him), acted as an arbitrator between the tribes at the age of 35, before prophethood had yet come to him. At that time a disagreement arose among the polytheist tribes of Mecca, who were building the Ka'ba, over who would set the Black Stone in its place, and they did not reach any agreement. Upon this the tribes all but decided to go to war among themselves. But at that moment, on the proposal of Abu Umayya ibn al-Mughira, the eldest of the Quraysh, it was decided that the first person to enter through the Banu Shayba gate of the Noble Sanctuary would be appointed arbitrator. Just then Muhammad came in from the awaited place. The people of Quraysh said with one voice: "This is a trustworthy (amin) person. We are content with the decision he will give." When the matter was explained to our Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), he at once took his cloak (rida) off his back and spread it on the ground. He put the Black Stone on the cloak. He had one person from each tribe hold the edges of the cloak and had it brought to the place where the stone was to be set. There he placed the stone in the wall of the Ka'ba with his own hand. The people of Quraysh were extremely pleased with the way Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) solved this problem.3 In this way the Prophet resolved a dispute and prevented a war.

An event of arbitration that is very well known to minds took place in the period of Ali (may Allah be pleased with him). In the history of Islam, at the Battle of Siffin, towards the end of the battle, there occurred the case of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari and Amr ibn al-As, who were chosen as arbitrators between Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) and Mu'awiya. Here, however, no positive result came out, because of the disagreement between the arbitrators.

In Anatolia too, in the southern and eastern regions, the disputes arising among people and family matters were mostly resolved by the "mela" and the "seyda", who are known there as scholars. "Mela/Molla" and "seyda" are the names given to our teachers who graduated from our classical madrasas. Problems of this kind have also been brought to a solution by the "sheikhs" present in the region. In addition the "aghas" have also played a role in disputes of this kind in the region.

In the particular sense, arbitration has been an institution that ensures the fulfilment of many duties with regard to the family. In the large and extended family structure that existed in history, this institution ensured mediation between families, truthful testimony to events and the conduct of their affairs for the sake of peace. It also strove to bring to a solution the disputes arising between spouses as a nuclear family. And if a divorce did take place between the spouses, it ensured, and ensures, that they part not as enemies at daggers drawn, separating resentfully with hatred and anger, but in a civilised manner, having settled the problems relating to their separation.

If arbitration between families does not take place, it will always be possible to witness the following negative results in society:

1- The families of the separated spouses become enemies of one another.

2- The resentment between the families of the separated spouses lasts for years, even for a lifetime.

3- The hatred and anger of the parties towards each other does not die down.

4- Some of the women who want a divorce are either left disabled or killed by their husbands.

5- Women who kill their husbands who want a divorce are also encountered.

6- Children grow up deprived of the love of one of the spouses (father or mother). This causes many psychological crises in children.

7- Whichever spouse the child stays with, the other spouse is mostly made "dead", killed although he or she has not died. This situation leaves very negative effects and marks on children.

8- While the children are in the custody of one of the spouses, the other spouse cannot see his or her own child; the child is kept away from the spouse or not shown. Thus the child itself is directly inside the problem and is crushed under the events.

9- The spouse who cannot see the child abducts his or her own child in retaliation against the other side, and thus the enmity between the parties goes on.

10- Or the child is raised by the spouse who has taken custody as an enemy of the other spouse. Thus the child, although not a party to the dispute at all, is unjustly dragged into being a party. This opens wounds in the child that cannot be made good, disturbs the child's psychology and drags the child into various crises.

In general it is also possible to call family arbitration the family council. Seen within this frame, it carries out a function that brings to a solution every kind of dispute that may arise in the family, watches over the rights of the parties and ensures that they make peace with one another.

This arbitration or family council, while realising calm and peace among families, the smallest unit of society, in fact ensures the calm of society. For societies made up of healthy families are, in the end, healthy and dynamic, and live in calm and peace.

Part I: The Question of Family Arbitration in Islam

A. Dictionary and Technical Meaning

a. Dictionary Meaning

Family arbitration (aile hakemliği) is a noun phrase made up of two words. Both are words of Arabic origin.

Family (aile): It is the community that has come together through lineage and marriage and is found under one roof. It is mostly made up of the mother, the father and the children, together with the grandmother and the grandfather.4 This definition rather denotes the nuclear family, the narrow family or the small family. Or it is the community made up of the wife, the husband and the unmarried children.5

A person's wife, relatives or household are also called family.6

The large family: It means all those between whom there is a tie of blood, the lineage. It is the whole formed by the kin and relatives living in the same house.7 This kind of family is also called the extended family. It is the family that includes, besides the mother, the father and the children, relatives such as the grandmother, the grandfather and the maternal and paternal uncles.8

In Turkish the following noun phrases relating to the family are frequently used: family friend, family life, family hearth, family home, family garden, head of the family, family budget, family migration, family relations, family planning, family law, sociology of the family, family medicine, family happiness, family council and family nest.

Arbitrator (hakem): The person whom the parties choose with authority and agree upon in order to resolve the dispute between them. The person who has the authority to choose and to decide. The person who directs various competitions and matches (football, wrestling, boxing and so on). Likewise a jury, the one who makes the assessment in a competition.9

Hakem (al-Hakam) is at the same time one of the names of Allah. In a more particular sense the expression "Shall I seek a judge other than Allah?"10 occurs in the Noble Qur'an.11 Within this frame the word hakem occurs in only two places in the Noble Qur'an.

In Turkish expressions such as "board of arbitrators, arbitral award and refereed journal" are used.

In Arabic "an old man" is also called hakem.12 Hakem, in other words, is "to strive in order to put right, or to hold back from something."13 "It is the person who will reconcile two adversaries and settle their case."14

Today there are sworn arbitrators designated by the courts. The decision given by these arbitrators is called an "arbitral award".

Judge (hâkim): It means the one who exercises his sovereignty, carries out his command, makes his word prevail, the sovereign.15

The most distinct difference between the judge and the arbitrator is that the sovereignty of the former is realised in all fields, whereas the decision of the arbitrator is valid in definite and limited matters.

b. Technical Meaning

Family arbitration needs to be expressed in a broad and in a narrow sense:

The family arbitrator in the broad sense: The person whom the members of a family, or the families that make up a large family, choose with authority and agree upon in order to resolve the dispute between them. In this position the duty of arbitrator is mostly performed by the eldest of the family, the head of the family or the wise person of the family.

The concept of the family council is also close to this meaning. Family council: The body that determines and carries out the common views of the members of the family.16

The family arbitrator in the narrow sense: The person or persons chosen with authority from the family (or from outside the family) and agreed upon in order to resolve the dispute between the spouses (wife and husband).

B. Its History

The question of family arbitration or the family council goes back very far, together with the history of humanity. It is a fact that the problems arising in those times were brought to a solution by the elders of the family, the leaders of the tribe or wise persons. Throughout history, in these small societies (tribe, clan and kin group), the decisions taken and the rulings given by the notables regarding problems were valid and were applied in society. In the societies in which there was a prophet, the prophets stood at the centre of the solution and resolved the disputes arising in the family and in society. In places where there was no prophet, knowledgeable and righteous persons charged by the prophet, the scholars, undertook this duty.

In Jewish and Christian societies too, their learned men, rabbis, priests and monks, were the party that solved problems of this kind. Even today, in the USA (United States of America) and in the countries of Europe, both the clergy in the churches (monks and priests) and the rabbis in the synagogues are in the position of confidants of families. They concern themselves with their intimate and very private problems and produce solutions to them.

In Buddhism, Manichaeism, Brahmanism, Hinduism, Zoroastrianism, Nestorianism, Shamanism and the other religions of Asia too, problems of this kind were solved by their clergy and learned men. We can say that these practices are still in force.17

In all the societies of the world practices of this kind have continued until today under different faces. For the nature of being a society makes such a solution necessary.

Today there is the "matter of arbitration" in international trade. These are legal agreements between states which provide for the appointment of an arbitrator when a dispute arises between the parties in matters such as commerce, purchase, sale and customs. Indeed within the country too, practices of this kind can be counted as having come into force under different aspects. As a result, by virtue of the law of arbitration, the decisions taken by these arbitrators are applied. The situation that we also name the ombudsman is the same.

In the Qur'an, which was sent down to Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him), the last prophet of Islam, this subject has been commanded in an explanatory manner by a noble verse. The scholars and mujtahids of Islam made many proposals for the application of this verse, formed the system of this practice and exercised ijtihad upon it. These ijtihads, decisions and fatwas, and the laws formed later, have reached us down to the present day. This subject has been treated in depth in the books of tafsir, of fiqh and of Ahkam al-Qur'an, and has shed light for us.

C. Family Arbitration in the Qur'an

In the Noble Qur'an, the divine book sent to humanity last of all, it is said:

"And if you fear a breach between the two (spouses), send an arbitrator from his family and an arbitrator from her family. If they both desire to set things right, Allah will bring about agreement between them. Surely Allah is All-Knowing, All-Aware."18

The disagreement between spouses comes about either through the nushuz/rebellion of the woman or through the oppression of the man. The man tries to treat the rebellion and obstinacy of the woman by the method of correction in three ways, as mentioned before this verse. When the man continues his oppression, or when one is unable to deal with the rebellion of the woman, or when the limits of Allah are not being observed in the marriage and a breach between them is feared, it is obligatory (fard) upon the believers who are guarantors, who think of their welfare and interest, to send an arbitrator from the family of each party.19

In Islam the institution of the family is sacred and carries great value in that it is the basic structure of society. For this reason Islam wishes to repair the structure of the family by putting new bricks into the cracks in it, so that it may be long-lived and may develop.

Here Islam shows the last remedy at the moment when a breach between husband and wife is feared. It asks that one arbitrator be sent from the family of the man and one arbitrator from the family of the woman.

These arbitrators must remain far from the emotional outbursts that spoil the relation between the spouses, from the memories left in the subconscious and from the events of life. At the same time they must also be far from the influences which spoil the atmosphere of their life, hinder relations and, because of their closeness to the spouses, cover over all the other pure feelings in their lives. Yes, by lending an ear to the demands of the two families, by showing compassion to the small children... By refraining from preferring one to the other, as happens in our time... By thinking of the safety of the family that is faced with ruin, of the children and of the spouses... In fact these two arbitrators are the most trustworthy persons to whom the family may confide its secrets. For they are from their own families. For this reason there is no harm in pouring out their secrets. Nor do these arbitrators have any interest in spreading the secrets they learn. Indeed they have a great interest in keeping them.

Both arbitrators are of one mind on setting the spouses right. Provided, of course, that there truly is a wish to get along in the hearts of the spouses... For anger alone is enough to hinder this wish. Allah the Exalted removes the discord between the spouses by means of the strong desire in the hearts of the arbitrators.20

The commentator Izzat Darwaza has stated that he could not reach any particular report about the occasion of the revelation of this verse.21 Furthermore, according to the view of the commentators, those addressed by this verse are the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), the spouses and those concerned. These concerned parties are the deputies of the Prophet, those in authority and the qadis.22 In the report that has come from Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) on this subject, he ordered that disputes between spouses be reported first to those in authority and then to their deputy, and said that those in authority have the right to intervene in disputes of this kind.23

In fact Allah has left open the question of who will choose the arbitrators. If the husband and wife wish to settle their dispute, they may each choose an arbitrator from among their own relatives. Or the heads of the families of the two sides may charge two arbitrators with resolving this matter. Or else, if the matter has been brought to the court, the court may appoint two arbitrators before it has yet taken any step. For this is a plan of reconciliation made before applying to the court or before taking the last step.24

Here it has been considered more fitting that the arbitrators be from among the relatives. The appointment of an arbitrator from outside the relatives is also permissible. The right to choose the arbitrator belongs first of all to the spouses. It is more commendable that this be done in consultation with the relatives of both sides. What will be the degree of authority of these arbitrators? Can they bring about both situations, uniting and separating? The mujtahids of Islam have differed on this subject. Some of them said that they can separate and that this is a talaq ba'in (final divorce), which has been reported from Ali (may Allah be pleased with him). And some said that the arbitrators are charged with uniting and cannot separate them, which has been reported from al-Hasan al-Basri (may Allah have mercy on him).25 According to the Hanafi and Shafi'i schools the arbitrators have no authority to bring the matter to a conclusion; they only have the authority to make attempts at reconciliation, of which it is not certain whether the spouses will accept them or not. The Caliph Uthman (may Allah be pleased with him) and the Caliph Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) appointed arbitrators who, according to the circumstances, had the authority to carry out the decision of reconciliation or separation. According to Abdullah ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him), Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, al-Sha'bi, Muhammad ibn Sirin and other jurists, the arbitrators have the authority to impose whatever they see fit (reconciliation or divorce).26

D. Family Arbitration in the Sunna and in the Period of the Companions

a. In the Period of the Prophet

We have not come across a report that an event of arbitration actually took place in the period of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him). However, the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) found solutions at once to the events or problems that arose in his own period, and applied them immediately. In any case, whenever there was any family matter among the Companions, they brought it to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him). And he, if a verse was being revealed, made it known, or, if no verse was revealed, carried out his own ruling. When we give two examples of this, the matter will be better understood.

One of the Companions, Abdullah ibn Umar (may Allah be pleased with him), one day divorces his wife while she is menstruating. The situation is brought to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) by his father Umar (may Allah be pleased with him); our Prophet orders that divorce cannot be carried out at the time of menstruation and that he must do it in the woman's time of purity, and says: "Order him to take his wife back, then let him keep her, within the marriage, until she becomes pure, then menstruates again and then becomes pure. After that, if he wishes let him keep her, and if he wishes let him divorce her before having intercourse. This is the waiting period in which Allah the Exalted has commanded that women be divorced."27 Upon this Abdullah ibn Umar (may Allah be pleased with him), obeying the order of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), gives up the divorce, waits for his wife's time of purity and divorces her after that.

Jamila (or Habiba), daughter of Abdullah son of Ubayy, who was the wife of Thabit ibn Qays, comes to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) because of the ugliness of her husband, or according to another report because he beat her, and asks him to separate her from her husband. On this subject she says:

"O Messenger of Allah! I have nothing to say against either the religion or the character of Thabit ibn Qays. But I fear ingratitude within Islam." The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) asked her:

"Will you give back his garden?" And she answered:

"Yes." Upon this the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said to Thabit:

"Accept the garden and divorce her!"28

Here the woman makes a request to be divorced from her husband. In Islamic law this procedure is given the name "mukhala'a". This means "the woman's release from the bond of marriage in return for a consideration". This lady, with the approval of the Prophet, dissolved the contract of marriage by giving back to her husband the garden she had received as mahr.

Al-Tirmidhi also records an event, relating this report from Rukana:

I said: "O Messenger of Allah, I have divorced my wife with the batta divorce (in a final manner)." He said:

"What did you intend?"

I said: "I intended one divorce."

He said: "By Allah?"

I said: "By Allah."

He said: "The divorce is whatever your intention was."29

This is the case of a Companion who divorced his wife with three divorces in one sitting; when it was brought to the Prophet, such a dialogue took place between them. As is seen in this hadith too, the Prophet counts three divorces made in one sitting as one divorce.

As in these three examples, family matters are conveyed to the Prophet, who is accepted as a judge or an arbitrator. And the Prophet gives his rulings on the subject and ensures that they are applied.

b. In the Period of the Companions

In the period of the Companions too, practices relating to family arbitration took place, especially during the rule of the Rightly Guided Caliphs. Let us try to make the matter understood by giving a few examples here.

The following event took place in the period of the second Caliph, Umar (may Allah be pleased with him).

A woman came to Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) and said:

"I have come to complain to you of the best of the people in the world. There is no other person in the world who does righteous deeds as he does. One can surpass him only by doing the deeds he does. He spends his night in worship and his day in fasting..." and after saying this, unable to say the rest out of modesty and shame, she said:

"O Commander of the Believers! Excuse me from completing this complaint!" And Umar said to her:

"May Allah give you your reward. You have praised your husband very beautifully. I excuse you."

After the woman had gone, Ka'b ibn Sur (may Allah be pleased with him) said:

"O Commander of the Believers! Even though that woman could not say the rest out of shame, in my opinion she clearly complained to you of someone." Upon Umar's asking:

"Well, of whom did she complain?" Ka'b said:

"She complained of her husband. For he does not observe the conjugal right, which is one of the rights his wife has over him." Upon this Umar ordered:

"Bring me that woman and her husband at once!" And after they had come he said to Ka'b:

"You settle their case."

Although Ka'b wanted to object, saying: "How can I act as arbitrator in a place where you are present?", Umar said:

"You must look into this case. For you understood at once what the woman meant, although I could not. Therefore to arbitrate between them is your right far more than mine." And Ka'b turned to the woman's husband and said:

"Allah the Exalted says: 'Marry such women as seem good to you, two, three or four' (al-Nisa 4/3). In that case fast for three days; and spend the fourth day with your wife without fasting. In the same way worship for three nights until morning; but spend the fourth night together with your wife and sleep beside her!"

Upon this ruling that he gave, Umar said:

"This that you have done is even finer than the first," and appointed him qadi of Basra.30

The third Caliph, Uthman (may Allah be pleased with him), and the fourth Caliph, Ali (may Allah be pleased with him), appointed arbitrators who, according to the circumstances, had the authority to carry out the decision of reconciliation or separation.31

When the matter of Aqil, the son of Abu Talib, and his wife Fatima (who was the daughter of Utba ibn Rabi'a) was brought to the court of Uthman (may Allah be pleased with him), Uthman (may Allah be pleased with him), the commander of the believers, appointed Ibn Abbas from the husband's family and Mu'awiya from the woman's family as arbitrators and gave them the authority, according to the circumstances, to reconcile or to separate.32 He said to these two arbitrators:

"If your opinion is for reconciliation, I will reconcile them. If it is for their separation, I will separate them."33

In the same way Ali (may Allah be pleased with him), in the period of his caliphate, appointed arbitrators in a situation similar to this and gave them the authority to reconcile or to separate the spouses.34

The example relating to this occurred as follows. Abd al-Razzaq, deriving it from the hadith of Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him), reported from Muhammad ibn Sirin, and he from Ubayda (may Allah be pleased with him), that he said:

One day I witnessed a woman and a man [between whom there was a dispute], each of whom had a group of people with them, coming to Ali (may Allah be pleased with him). Upon this Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) appointed an arbitrator from the woman's family and an arbitrator from the man's family. Then he called the arbitrators before him and said to them:

"Do you know what the duty that falls to you is? What you must do is this: if you unite the spouses, I too will unite them. (In another report: if you separate the spouses, I too will separate them.)" The woman, hearing this, said:

"Whether it be for me or against me, I am content with the ruling in the Book of Allah." And upon this the husband said:

"If it is separation, no, I do not accept." Ali (may Allah be pleased with him), hearing this, said:

"You have lied. I swear by Allah that you cannot leave here until you are content with the ruling in the Book of Allah, even if it is against you."35

E. The View of the Schools of Fiqh on the Question of Family Arbitration

"And if you fear a breach between the two (spouses), send an arbitrator from his family and an arbitrator from her family. If they both desire to set things right, Allah will bring about agreement between them. Surely Allah is All-Knowing, All-Aware."36

In accordance with this noble verse all the mujtahids of Islam have united on the ruling that, in the case of discord, the judge, representing the community or the state, shall send the said two arbitrators and ask them to make an attempt at setting things right.

While the schools of fiqh have agreed on the matter of reconciling the spouses, they have differed on the authority of the arbitrators. Two basic views have emerged on this subject:

1- According to Abu Hanifa, and according to one report Imam al-Shafi'i and Ahmad ibn Hanbal, the arbitrators are in the position of agents. Only those acts of disposal for which their principals have given authority are valid; they cannot divorce of their own accord; for divorcing, apart from the exceptions in the text, belongs to the husband.

2- According to Imam Malik, and according to the other report Ahmad ibn Hanbal, and mujtahids such as Ali, Ibn Abbas, al-Sha'bi, Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, al-Awza'i, Muhammad ibn Sirin and Sa'id ibn Jubayr, the arbitrators are in the position of judges. Without needing the husband and wife to grant them agency, the choice of one of the options of setting right, or separation with or without a consideration, belongs to them. The said verse and the practice of the Companions indicate this."37

F. Family Arbitration in the Ottoman Decree on Family Law of 1917

The first law of Islamic family law in the modern sense is the "Hukuk-ı Aile Kararnâmesi" (Decree on Family Law) codified in the Ottoman Empire in 1917. This decree remained in force for a long time in some Islamic countries after the Ottomans too, and served as a model for the family laws prepared later.

Since the Mecelle did not cover branches of private law such as the law of persons, family law and the law of inheritance, this field was again left to the books of fiqh and fatwa, and this gave rise to certain difficulties and problems, especially for judges. A commission was set up to fill the gap, and the draft prepared by the said commission, based on fiqh, taking contemporary laws as a model in respect of form and including, beside the Hanafi school, some rulings of the other schools as well, was put into force as a decree under the name "Hukuk-ı Aile Kararnâmesi" on 25 October 1917.38 The decree, which remained in force until 19 June 1919, was repealed on that date as a result of certain pressures from within and from without.39

The family decree of 1917, the first product of the movement of legislating and codifying our family law, consists of 157 articles and is divided into two books called munakahat and mufaraqat. The book of munakahat is made up of six main sections and the book of mufaraqat of three main sections.40

This decree was arranged by making use of principles such as that rulings may change with the change of the times and that the ruling of the school preferred by the head of state shall be applied;41 its articles are in keeping with the spirit of Islam.

The commission on personal status which met in Istanbul on 2 May 1923 worked to prepare a new law. The decisions which this commission took through its work and deliberations were published in the Adliye Ceridesi (Journal of Justice), year 1339.

A second commission, which met for the same purpose between the years 1924 and 1925, prepared a draft as the result of its work. Many provisions of this draft are the same as those of the Decree on Family Law dated 1917. The draft has 142 articles.42

The idea of adopting one of the Western codes as the Turkish Civil Code grew very strong in 1925, and finally one day the duties of the said commissions were brought to an end with a communication43 which the Minister of Justice of the time, M. Esat Bozkurt, made before them. The Swiss Civil Code, having been translated as a whole with some amendments, was adopted on 17 February 1926 and entered into force on 6 October 1926.44

Article 130 of the Decree on Family Law, which relates to the arbitrators, regulated ill-treatment and discord as follows:

"If dispute and discord appear between the spouses and one of the two parties applies to the judge, the judge appoints one arbitrator from the family of each of the two parties. If no one to be appointed arbitrator can be found from the family of one or of both parties, or if one is found but does not possess the qualities to be an arbitrator, he appoints suitable persons from outside. The family council formed in this way examines the statements and defences of the two parties and strives to set things right between them. Where this is not possible, if the fault lies with the husband it separates them. And if it lies with the wife it effects a mukhala'a upon the whole or a part of the mahr. If the arbitrators cannot agree, the judge appoints another board of arbitrators possessing the required qualities, or a third arbitrator who has no kinship with the two parties. The ruling that the arbitrators give is final and not open to objection."

Article 131: "...the ruling issued for separation comprises a talaq ba'in, and the matter is registered in accordance with the procedure."

The law, by giving the board of arbitrators which it calls the "family council" the authority to set right and to divorce, without being open to objection, adhered to the Maliki school. The taking of a ruling belonging to the Maliki school in the Ottoman community, where the Hanafi school was dominant, was defended in the statement of reasons of the said law.45

G. Family Arbitration in Egypt and the Other Islamic Countries (Personal Status)

The Decree on Family Law remained in force for a long time in Islamic countries such as Syria, Iraq and Lebanon. Other, local laws adopted in Syria and Iraq took its place, while in Lebanon it has continued in force until today.46

The Egyptian Civil Code too applied Islamic family law / Personal Status (Ahwal Shakhsiyya), and it has continued in force until today.

The Syrian Civil Code, on the question of family arbitration, adopted in its articles 112 to 115 the same basis as the points in the Decree on Family Law, with some differences and in a more detailed manner:

"It is permissible for a woman who complains of her husband, who treats her badly by word or deed and makes life unbearable, to request separation from him. If it is established that he causes harm and the judge does not succeed in setting things right between them, he separates them, and this is a ba'in divorce. If ill-treatment and harm are not established, or if the claimant is the husband, the judge gives them a respite of not less than one month to come to terms and be reconciled. If the claimant insists and no agreement has come about, the judge appoints two arbitrators, one from the family of the wife and the other from the family of the man. Where this is not possible the judge appoints as arbitrators persons in whom he finds the ability to set things right, and has them swear that they will perform their duties with honesty and justice." "The arbitrators first investigate the causes of the dispute and discord, then gather the husband and wife and the persons whose presence they consider necessary in a session presided over by the judge. Part II: Basic Issues in Family Arbitration

A. The Conditions That Make Arbitration Necessary

Marriage is, in its essence, a voluntary partnership between the spouses.

Marriage is, in its essence, a voluntary partnership between the spouses. There are certain rules and principles that this partnership brings. Just as the husband and the wife have rights over each other, there are also rights that the parties must observe. When these rights are not protected, not observed and are trampled upon, the affection, respect and compassion between the spouses decrease. That these conditions, which form the foundation of marriage, begin to decrease causes quarrels and unrest to begin and the pillar of the family to collapse. It is in this situation that the following conditions appear in the family, negatively and concretely:

1- The man continues his ill-treatment and oppression.

2- The man turns away from the woman, is ill-tempered and does not fulfil his responsibilities towards her.

3- The woman rebels against her husband, is ill-tempered and does not fulfil her responsibilities towards him.

4- The woman does not fulfil and does not protect the trusts belonging to her husband.

5- The limits of Allah that must exist in marriage are not observed by both or by one of the spouses.

6- Because of great quarrels and unrest, a breach between the spouses is feared.

7- The dispute is at its peak, and hatred, anger and hostility come about between the spouses.

8- The love, mercy, respect and compassion between the spouses disappear to a degree that shakes a life together.

When these situations and conditions appear, an effort is made to remove the unrest between the spouses, to give them counsel and to reconcile them, by one person from the family of each party, from among authorised and competent persons, becoming an arbitrator.

H. Who Can Be a Family Arbitrator? The Qualities of the Arbitrator

The family arbitrator must, first of all, be someone from the family of the spouses. For the persons who will be charged as arbitrators, since they are their relatives, know the parties very well, are acquainted with their family life, know the hidden sides of the family and have the ability to understand the demands and preferences of the spouses in the best way.

It is easier and more fitting for arbitrators who are relatives to determine who is committing ill-treatment and oppression, who has plunged into rebellion and sin, and where the injustice comes from. For people open up more to the relatives who they think will keep their secrets, and can pour out their hearts at ease. They can easily share their private matters and their secrets with them. In any case the arbitrators will not tell these secrets to anyone. Indeed there is no benefit at all in their telling them.

What is to be done when there is no such authorised and competent person within the family of the parties? Will this problem be left just as it is and remain in suspense? Of course in answer to this question we say no. Islam, as a religion, never leaves its adherents in a void and in suspense. Islamic law has brought a solution to this too. In that case persons who have these qualifications will carry out the family arbitration. Who can these persons be today? This question may be answered as follows:

1- He must be a jurist or specialist in Islamic law.

2- Scholars who know Islamic family law and the life of society well.

3- Opinion leaders, muftis, meles and hodjas who know Islamic family law and the life of society well.

4- Wise and experienced persons who know the life of society and of the family well. These persons, even if they do not know Islamic law, can bring sound solutions because they know very well the rights, duties and responsibilities of the spouses towards each other.

5- Psychologists who know Islamic family law and the life of society well.

These persons are preferred according to the order above. Those who must be charged with this matter first of all are the specialists in Islamic law. If they are not available, the others are appointed as arbitrators, observing the order above.

What Are the Qualities of the Arbitrator:

The arbitrators who will be charged with protecting the structure of the family, which forms the cornerstone of society, must of course have certain characteristics. Ordinary people cannot be charged with so important a duty. For this reason we can list the qualities that must be found in arbitrators as follows:

1- He must be Muslim, free, of sound mind and of age.

2- The arbitrator must be married.

3- The arbitrator must be someone from the family.

4- If no competent arbitrator is found from the family, he must be an authorised and competent person from outside.

5- He must be a person of justice.

6- He must be a person of good morals and have an orderly life.

7- He must be someone trustworthy who keeps secrets.

8- He must be someone far-sighted and discerning who knows family law and the relations between spouses.

It is not right or fitting for persons who do not carry these qualities and conditions in themselves to be arbitrators. They bring harm instead of benefit, and perhaps cause situations to arise that cannot be repaired.

I. Who May Request an Arbitrator?

When family life between the spouses becomes unbearable and the unrest reaches advanced dimensions, it will be necessary to request an arbitrator for the safety and peace of the family. But from whom will this request come? A request for an arbitrator coming from which side should be put into process and assessed? Who will have a say on this subject? These questions need to be answered. For in the noble verse the sending of an arbitrator has been commanded, but the question of who will request the arbitrator has not been made clear. We too can state that, as the result of our reflections, we have reached the following conviction, and that the order should be as follows.

1- One of the spouses, the wife or the husband, may request an arbitrator.

2- Both of the spouses together may request an arbitrator.

3- The family of one of the spouses, the mother, the father or the siblings, may request an arbitrator.

4- The families of both of the spouses together may request an arbitrator.

5- The close family friends of the spouses may request an arbitrator.

6- The close neighbours and confidants of the spouses may request an arbitrator.

7- The court, intervening in the event that has been brought before it, may request an arbitrator.

The mujtahids of Islam, taking into account the wording "if you fear..."50 that occurs in the noble verse, have reached different ijtihads on this subject. Al-Suddi stated that the wife and the husband may appoint the arbitrators themselves. Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him), Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Imam al-Shafi'i, on the other hand, said that the one addressed by it is the ruling authority/the caliph. And Imam Malik stated that both the ruling authority and the families or guardians of the spouses may make the request for an arbitrator on this subject.51

J. The Position of the Arbitrator

The position of the family arbitrator has been much debated among the mujtahids of Islam. Is the arbitrator a witness, an agent or a mediator? Or is he authorised only in the matter of reconciliation, or else authorised both in the matter of reconciliation and in that of separation? Different ijtihads have emerged on these subjects. Let us try to see them in order.

1- Is the Arbitrator a Witness?

Some mujtahids have stated that the arbitrator is a witness. They say that they are witnesses to the disputes between husband and wife and must present their proposals for a solution on this subject and bring the matter to the court. It has been reported that al-Hasan al-Basri, Ibn Zayd, Imam al-Shafi'i and Imam Abu Hanifa are of this view.52 They are of the view that they have no compelling effect whatever between the spouses in the matter of uniting or separating them. Although the view that the arbitrators are witnesses has been reported in a weak form from Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him), what is correct according to him is the view that the arbitrators are not witnesses but true arbitrators.53

2- Is the Arbitrator an Agent?

The agent is the person who does the work with which he has been charged in the name of the principal. The most important point about the agent is that he cannot decide. For him to be able to decide he must be specially authorised. Thus he only does the duty given and reports to the principal on that subject. To act outside this is not right, nor is it legally valid.

If Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) had seen the arbitrators as agents in the period of his own caliphate, he would not have put such a question to them:

He had said: "Do you know what your duties require? If you unite them, I too will unite them. If you separate them, I too will separate them."

3- Is the Arbitrator a Mediator, a Family Therapist and a Conciliator?

In essence the duty of mediation and conciliation is present in the arbitrators. However, mediation is not an authority that decides. They are in the position of envoys of peace who strive to reconcile the parties and to bring them to terms. If the parties make peace, they have completed their duty. But if they do not make peace, there is nothing they can do.

4- Is the Arbitrator a Counsellor, a Family Therapist or a Psychologist?

The arbitrators are in the position of counsellors, even of psychologists, for the spouses. However, counselling and the work of a psychologist are quite different procedures.

Counsellors are persons who give information on the subjects on which they are consulted and offer recommendations and advice. They show the right way and the methods to the persons who consult them. Counsellors have no duty of sanction, nor do they have such a power in any case.

Psychologists, for their part, have a status different from that of counsellors. They try to treat persons whose psychology has been disturbed, who are spiritually uneasy and who display unbalanced behaviour. By determining the psychological make-up of that person, his demands and preferences and his social relations, they try to produce solutions suited to him. They produce these solutions only within a doctor-patient relationship.

Family therapists too display work that guides the family, tries to put the relations between the spouses in order, aims at having the spouses live in a peaceful family environment and establishes peace and trust. Rather than sanction, they strive for the realisation of peace between the spouses and for there to be no separation. They work as a kind of institution that supports the family.

5- The Authority of the Arbitrators to Decide

We had stated before that the mujtahids of Islam have differed on the authority of the arbitrators to decide and on how far the limits of this authority extend.

When we look at the functions that the arbitrators perform, we observe that between the spouses they act as witnesses, as mediators and also represent the family by agency. Beyond all these, he is in the position of an authority that decides whether the family will continue or not.

When we examine both the dictionary meaning of the word hakem and the meaning in which it is used in the Qur'an, we see that he is in the position of one "who has authority, who gives decisions and rulings, who has the authority to choose and to decide".

Hafiz Ibn Kathir has reported that "there is consensus (ijma') that the arbitrators have the authority to decide on the continuation of the marriage or on its being ended".54

In any case the efforts at setting right and reconciling aim at correcting a matter. To make a correction and put matters in order without authority is most of the time not possible. For this reason the aim of correction brings with it the authority to be given to the persons who will make the correction.

When the conviction arises in the arbitrators that the marriage will work, they make arrangements in keeping with this and, beside their counsel and recommendations, put certain decisions into practice.

But when the strong presumption arises that the marriage will not work, that the bond of marriage has been completely shaken, that the continuation of the marriage has been determined to bring evil and harm to the parties, and that in the continuation of the marriage the spouses will plunge into sin and rebellion by not respecting each other's rights, they decide on the separation of the spouses. With this procedure too the arbitrators take the path of correction; they ensure that the parties separate in a civilised and fine manner, without harming each other, without becoming enemies and without attempting murder. As can be seen, in both cases the arbitrators help the spouses in the finest way.

K. The Procedure for Applying Arbitration

The procedure for applying arbitration takes place within a definite frame, in order, as follows:

The arbitrators chosen by the family first of all meet with the spouses. The arbitrator of the woman's side meets with the woman, and the arbitrator of the man's side with the man. They try to learn the causes and the real sources of the unrest and disputes which shake the family deeply and endanger the unity of the family. Meanwhile they do not hold back from counselling the spouses towards the continuation of the marriage. They give recommendations on the importance of peace, on the spouses respecting each other's rights, on love and respect existing between them and on compassion and mercy not being abandoned.

After the arbitrators have listened to the spouses separately, they determine their observations, the causes of the dispute and the circumstances that prevent the marriage from working, and deliberate between themselves. After this both of the arbitrators together listen, in the same sitting, first to each one singly and then to the husband and wife together in the same sitting.

After the arbitrators have listened to the spouses, they again discuss and deliberate the situation between themselves, and if there is in addition a point that needs to be known they investigate it. They make every effort in their power for the spouses to be reconciled and to carry on their married life.

If the arbitrators decide on the continuation of the marriage and the reconciliation of the parties, the spouses comply with this decision. Taking into account also the counsel and recommendations given to them, they carry on their marriage by respecting each other's rights. By obeying the decision of the arbitrators the spouses both earn reward and keep their marriage standing.

If the conviction of the arbitrators weighs towards the separation of the spouses, but there is hope of reconciliation between them, they grant the spouses a period of at most about three months, on condition that it be not less than one month. At the end of this period the arbitrators listen to the spouses in the same sitting while the two are together. Then they assess what they have heard. If the conviction of the arbitrators is in favour of the continuation of the marriage they decide on its continuation, and if they must separate, on their separation. This decision is applied by the court and officially registered.

1- The Parties, or One of Them, Do Not Agree to the Appointment of an Arbitrator

Although dispute, discord, conflict and violence between the spouses have reached their peak, they may not agree to the request for and appointment of an arbitrator. If both or one of the spouses does not want the appointment of an arbitrator, what will need to be done in that case?

When we look at the noble verse in relation to this subject, it says, addressing us: "And if you fear a breach between the two (spouses), send an arbitrator from his family and an arbitrator from her family."55 Allah the Exalted, by the noble verse, makes it obligatory (fard) upon the legally responsible Muslims to send an arbitrator in these situations. And this will be done by the closest relatives of the spouses, their confidants and friends, or the close neighbours who are witnesses to the matter.

If one or both of the spouses do not accept the arbitrators sent by the families, the court intervenes in the situation. From then on the parties have no right to object to the arbitrators appointed by the court.

The court, according to the decision that the arbitrators will take, registers the status of the spouses and brings the matter to an end.

2- The Court Does Not Apply the Decision of the Arbitrators

The arbitrators may give a decision of reconciliation or of separation concerning a family in which there is dispute and disagreement. But if the court does not apply this decision, what will the way to a solution be?

When the court does not wish to apply the decision of the two arbitrators, it appoints beside these arbitrators a third arbitrator whom it will itself determine. Together with the new arbitrator the former arbitrators listen to the spouses again and reach a positive or negative decision about the family. From then on this decision is applied by the court and the legal result is registered.

3- The Arbitrators Divorce, the Court Does Not

The decision that the arbitrators give concerning the family is very important. Decisions relating to reconciliation do not pose much of a problem for the spouses and the families. But what must be done if the court does not apply the decision of the arbitrators relating to divorce and does not accept the divorce?

What will become of this decision of separation, will it remain in suspense? Of course the status of the spouses remaining in suspense causes them to suffer harm because they cannot fulfil their own rights. And this is a situation that the law cannot accept. What, then, must the court do?

In such a situation the court may follow two paths:

1- The court appoints a third arbitrator to the existing board of arbitrators. The board of three arbitrators discusses and assesses the matter anew. From then on the decision taken by this board is applied by the court and the legal result is registered.

2- Or, if the court finds the work of the arbitrators lacking and is convinced that the decisions taken and their grounds are insufficient, it itself appoints a second board of arbitrators.

4- The Court Divorces, the Arbitrators Do Not

The arbitrators, after completing their meetings with the spouses, their hearings and their assessments, may rule for the continuation of the marriage. But what must be done if the court wishes to divorce the spouses although the arbitrators have reached the decision that the marriage should continue and that there is no need for divorce?

In fact, when we examine the matter in depth, we can see that the role of the family arbitrators is at a more advanced level than that of the court. For the family arbitrators, by reason of their position and competence, are the persons who have the quality of knowing the spouses best. It is not possible for the bench of the court to know the spouses at this level and to penetrate the events in depth. They will give judgment only upon confessions and testimonies. For the most part the spouses do not disclose the truths in the courts, or do not say them on the grounds that they are private. Indeed, even if the spouses have said these private matters, it is not possible for the court to carry out the legal substantiation that would prove them. In essence it is legally not possible to substantiate the events that pass between two persons.

It is the institution of family arbitration that has the quality of being able to close this gap that exists in the courts. The family arbitrators obtain the opportunity to meet with the spouses one to one in sincere settings and to penetrate the matter. The courts do not have such a chance.

For this reason it is not a right practice for the court to divorce a family that the arbitrators have not divorced.

L. The Legal Result of Arbitration

In our opinion the legal result of arbitration should be as follows:

Since the arbitrators are chosen from among the competent and trustworthy persons of the family, their chance of seeing the family life of the spouses, their demands and preferences, their faults and who is acting unjustly and badly is higher. And because they know the family closely, the proposals for a solution that they bring will be on a scale that ensures the health and permanence of the family, or their separation in kindness and in a civilised manner.

Whether peace is secured by the arbitrators and there is union, or whether there is separation, the safety and calm of the family will be realised, and matters will proceed more securely, easily and comfortably.

For this reason the decisions taken by the family arbitrators will be sounder decisions, more suited to the structure of the family. Therefore it is necessary first of all that the spouses comply with these decisions and that the families of the spouses adapt to them. In applying the decisions of the arbitrators the families must take more active roles and make matters easier. And after the decision they must not give occasion for the atmosphere to grow tense, paying no heed to gossip, loose talk and turned-up noses.

The courts too must apply these decisions at once and carry out the procedures of official registration.

M. The Application of Islamic Family Arbitration in Secular Countries

Is Islamic family arbitration applied in secular (laik) countries, or is it not applied? Our answer to this question will be in the affirmative. Let us try to see with examples what kind of practices are carried out in this regard, both in laicist countries and in secular ones.

The Model of India

India is a country that applies secularism in its administration. That the majority of its population belong to another religion has brought the principle that different laws are also applied in the country. For the Muslim population of about 200 million living in the country, Islamic family law is applied. Family courts of Islamic law have also been set up in the judicial system. In this way Muslims bring their matters of marriage, divorce, inheritance and what relates to them to a solution in these courts.

The USA (United States of America)

The USA and some countries of Europe, even though there is no principle of laicism in their constitutions, carry out secular practices and are engaged in explicit practices towards religions. That is, they wish to be open and clear towards religions and to preserve their neutral position.

For example, in the USA today the contract of marriage is made in churches and synagogues; these contracts are valid and are officially accepted, and the state only registers the legal result. There again the authority to perform the contract of marriage, which is a principle of Islamic family law, has been given to the imams of mosques and to the sheikhs in the dervish lodges. The Jerrahi, Naqshi, Bektashi, Shadhili, Rifa'i, Mevlevi and other lodges in the USA have been given authority in these matters. This authorisation is made by the official authorities and is likewise accepted by them.

The municipalities perform the contract of marriage for those who do not make a religious contract of marriage.56 The esteemed jurist of Islamic law Prof. Dr. Yusuf Ziya Kavakçı, who lived in the USA for many years and carried out university work there, has stated that it is possible to establish Islamic courts in the United States.57

In Europe

The "Sharia Courts in England", which came onto the agenda in the media of Turkey three years ago, continue to be applied in England at present as well. Of course in courts where Islamic law is valid, family law will also be applied. Family arbitration, which forms a part of family law, will also keep its force.

In one state in Germany the Minister of Justice has announced to the whole world that he has need of judges of sharia. Indeed he has stated that in some courts the cases of Muslims are heard by judges of Islamic law. An example of this is also given: the first wife of a husband who had two wives and died brought a case before the court in Germany and requested that the retirement pension of her husband be assigned to her alone. But the court that heard the case, since having two wives is permitted in Islamic law, rejected the case of the first wife and assigned the pension to both wives.

Conclusion

In protecting the family, family arbitration is an institution that has been necessary and useful in every period of societies and has served as an insurance.

In protecting the family, family arbitration is an institution that has been necessary and useful in every period of societies and has served as an insurance. To bring this institution into being and keep it alive is a protective system that will guard the family from many dangers and will prevent divorces, or at least reduce them.

The sound application of this system in society, in accordance with its rules, will also eliminate or reduce to the lowest level the negative results that may arise after divorce, such as resentment, hatred and anger, loathing, murder, maiming and wounding.

Moreover, the application of this system is necessary also because the great majority of society is Muslim. For Muslims carry out all their affairs in the world according to the commands and prohibitions of the religion. Where this is not so, they are prevented from living their own beliefs. And this runs contrary to principles secured in the constitutions, such as "freedom of religion and belief" and "performing acts of worship while applying the commands of the religion".

Family arbitration was applied in history in all Islamic countries. In the last period it was applied in the Ottoman State as well. This institution also remained in force for a long time in Egypt, Syria, Iraq and Lebanon, within the scope of family law.

Today the Muslims living in the USA and, in Europe, in England and in some states of Germany, resolve their family matters in the courts there according to the rulings of the religion of Islam. All of the Muslims living in India settle their problems in the Islamic family courts established there for them. To be treated according to the requirements of their religion and beliefs is the most natural right of all human beings.

Since there are practices of this kind in laicist and secular countries such as the USA, England and Germany, there should be no obstacle whatever to the application of these laws in Turkey, 99 % of whose people are Muslim.

In conclusion, we propose that the laws of the system of family arbitration be in the following form:

Law on Family Arbitration in Protecting the Family:

Article 1- If dispute, discord and conflict between the spouses reach their peak,

a- if both of the spouses together apply for the appointment of an arbitrator,

b- if one of the spouses, the wife or the husband, requests the appointment of an arbitrator,

c- if a relative of the spouses in the first degree requests the appointment of an arbitrator,

d- if a close confidant or neighbour of the spouses requests the appointment of an arbitrator,

the judge appoints one arbitrator from the family of each of the parties.

Article 2- If the judge cannot find anyone to be appointed arbitrator from the family of one party or of both parties, or if one is found but does not carry the qualities to be an arbitrator, he appoints from outside persons who are suited to arbitration and in whom he finds the ability to set things right.

Article 3- The family council/board of arbitrators that is formed meets with the spouses, listens to them, takes their defences and investigates the causes of the dispute and discord. It also hears the claims and the witnesses, if any, and spends all its efforts to reconcile the spouses and tries to bring them to peace.

Article 4- When the arbitrators lose hope of reconciling the spouses, if the whole or the greater part of the wrong and the fault lies with the husband, they separate them with one ba'in divorce.

Article 5- If the whole or the greater part of the fault lies with the woman, they rule for separation by way of mukhala'a upon the whole or a part of the mahr.

Article 6- If it cannot be decided with which of the spouses the fault and the wrong lie more, in that case, if the arbitrators cannot reconcile the spouses, they separate the parties (annulment, faskh).

Article 7- If the arbitrators disagree,

a- the judge adds a third arbitrator who has no kinship with the parties,

b- or he appoints a new board of arbitrators who carry the qualities.

Article 8- The decisions given by the arbitrators are final and cannot be objected to. The judge registers this decision and makes it into a judgment.
