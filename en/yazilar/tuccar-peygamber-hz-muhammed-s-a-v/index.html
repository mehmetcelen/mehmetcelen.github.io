The Merchant Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him)

December 1, 2018About 6 min read

0:00

Read by an artificial voice. Arabic passages and footnotes are not read.

Translated from the Turkish original.Read the original

My dear readers,

In this article we will dwell on the merchant life of the Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him), who was sent as a mercy to the worlds and is the last link in the chain of the prophets. As is known, the prophets are the chosen and precious servants of Allah. Both before and after their prophethood they are under the protection of Allah. Our Creator, who made the human being precious, valued us and sent us both books and prophets. He also left people free on the point of believing or not believing in His religion. Whoever wishes believes and whoever wishes does not believe; no hardship or pressure is put on anyone in this matter.

The life of the Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) before his prophethood passed as that of a very orderly, calm, courteous and trustworthy personality. Until the age of eight he grew up beside his grandfather Abd al-Muttalib, the powerful leader of Quraysh, and after that he was brought up by his uncle Abu Talib, the leader of the Banu Hashim. When the Prophet was 12 years old, he set out on a journey to Syria with his uncle Abu Talib, who was engaged in trade. At that time the powerful and wealthy families of Mecca exported to and imported from the land of Syria in the north and the land of Yemen in the south. These trade caravans earned large sums of money. The power of the tribe of Quraysh in the Arabian Peninsula also came from running this economic wheel very well.

After the age of 20, our Prophet began to work in the company of Khadija (may Allah be pleased with her), a wealthy businesswoman who owned a powerful trade caravan of Mecca. In a way he took on the duty of general manager or CEO there. After he came to this post, very great increases took place in the earnings of Khadija (may Allah be pleased with her). The Prophet carried out the exports and imports with Syria in the north and Yemen in the south. He sold in Syria the goods they took from Mecca, bought there what was needed, brought it to Mecca and sold it here. In this way a gain was made in both directions. This situation continued until prophethood came. Thus he provided for his household with a comfortable income. After he married his employer Khadija (may Allah be pleased with her) at the age of twenty-five, his status also changed. He had now become friends with the wealthy people of Mecca as well. These wealthy people were Abu Bakr, Uthman ibn Affan, Talha ibn Ubaydullah, al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf (may Allah be pleased with them) and the like.

The Prophet had six children from Khadija (may Allah be pleased with her), four daughters and two sons. He brought them up well and married off the daughters. When prophethood came fifteen years later, the first to believe in this prophet was our mother Khadija (may Allah be pleased with her), the wealthy employer. Then came his other wealthy merchant friends, first of all Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him). These merchants, Khadija first of all, spent their wealth in the way of Allah, following in the footsteps of the Prophet. Indeed, when Khadija (may Allah be pleased with her) and Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) passed away, they had nothing left to leave as an inheritance. For this wealth had melted away in the Prophet's activities of calling and conveying the message and in the affairs of the Muslims. They used to compete with one another in spending and giving charity for the sake of Allah.

Islam was organised in Mecca under the leadership of prophethood with the spending of these merchants. The Prophet, too, spent all that he had gained for the Muslims and for the work of the call. So much so that when he was about to emigrate to Medina, he took the camel from Abu Bakr on credit.

Thus the foundations of the Islamic economy, which led the Muslims for centuries, were laid in Medina.

After our Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) emigrated to Medina, he saw the economic market of the Jews and the market of the polytheists. He at once chose a place suitable for trade and opened the economic market of the Muslims. He asked that no tax be taken there and that goods be offered for sale in a proper way. Thus the market of the Muslims developed greatly and trade reached advanced dimensions. For the inspection of this market he appointed Umar ibn al-Khattab and a woman Companion. He himself went out to inspect from time to time. One day, when he plunged his hand into a sack of wheat, what should he find: the top was dry and the bottom was damp. When he asked the seller the reason for this, he received an answer that did not convince him. Thereupon he said: "Whoever deceives us is not one of us." That is to say, such a person has moved away from the way of the Prophet and from the Muslims' manner of conduct. For this reason, to deceive has been counted as forbidden, and to let oneself be deceived as a sin.

Our Prophet laid down very important golden rules concerning trade. Thus the foundations of the Islamic economy, which led the Muslims for centuries, were laid in Medina. He also praised those who engage in trade and said: "The honest and trustworthy merchant is with the prophets, the truthful and the martyrs."

The Muslim merchants who held these rules in the highest esteem walked in the way of the Prophet and spread the religion of Islam to the four corners of the world. It was at the hands of trustworthy and honest merchants that Islam was accepted in Indonesia, Malaysia, India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, the Philippines, Moro, Myanmar, Turkestan and many other regions. Today in Asia more than a billion Muslims in total have entered Islam through the honest dealings and the chaste conduct of merchants. In Africa, Somalia, Abyssinia (today's Ethiopia) and some of the countries of North Africa also chose Islam at the hands of merchants. In conclusion, the majority of the Muslim population, which is approaching two billion today, has been honoured with Islam through the good morals, the chastity, the upright conduct and the call of merchants.

As can be seen, this beautiful example is an example that today's Muslim merchants must also follow. When we look at it from this angle, great duties fall to them. They will both trade and secure their earnings in the halal way, and by acting honestly they will fulfil the duty of calling to Islam. How happy are the trustworthy merchants! How happy are those who strive for halal earnings!

In the hope of meeting in our next article,

With my greetings, love and respect…
