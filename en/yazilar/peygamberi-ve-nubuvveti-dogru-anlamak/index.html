Understanding the Prophet and Prophethood Correctly

December 1, 2018About 10 min read

Translated from the Turkish original.Read the original

Throughout history the prophets have taken on a most exalted duty and have been the essential bearers who carried to humanity the divine messages that bring the human being together with the Transcendent Being. While carrying out this duty they endured every kind of persecution, torture and hardship with patience, and under no condition did they hold back from carrying out the duty of prophethood.

From the first prophet Adam (peace be upon him) to the last prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him), all the prophets conveyed the same religion, Islam, and taught it to people in full. They fulfilled the duty of conveying the message, of teaching and of purifying souls.

Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him), the last link of this blessed chain, left to us, humanity, two important and great miracles. The first of them is the Noble Qur'an, the divine message. The Noble Qur'an is the exalted book which the first three generations delivered to us unchanged and uncorrupted. These first three golden generations are the noble Companions, who were with the last prophet, the Followers (Tabi'un), who were with the Companions, and the Followers of the Followers (Atba' al-Tabi'in), who were with the Followers.

This exalted book is the mushaf that is the same everywhere today, in Asia, Europe, Africa, America and Australia, and that is kept in memory by tens of thousands of hafizes. This miracle is our source of life. It is the source of guidance and light that shows humanity the way, leads to what is right, and brings to the light of day good and evil, darkness and light, truth and falsehood. It is the fundamental rule of the Muslim's belief and mentality.

The second is the miracle of the civilisation of Islam. Together with conveying the fundamentals of the religion, Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) taught them to the Muslims with his life and his practice, and became the finest model for humanity. Although the establishment of this civilisation was carried on for centuries, it was presented to us in the most detailed and comprehensive form in his 23 years of prophethood. The bearers of this civilisation, too, were again the first three generations.

In the history of the prophets, the life and the daily relations of Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) were recorded down to every detail, and the noble Companions passed these records on to those who came after within a definite discipline. The life, the deeds and the words of the Prophet began both to be written down and to be memorised while he was still alive. Already in the first century of the Hijra the written texts of the "Sahifa of Hammam ibn Munabbih" and the like entered the service of the believers. The outstanding efforts that the Companions showed in compiling, collecting and transmitting the hadiths of the Prophet were the means by which these were carried to our day. They passed on not only the texts of these hadiths but also their chains of transmission in the finest way, and these hadiths, which came with a chain of transmission, formed the basic codes of the civilisation of Islam. Imam al-Shafi'i (may Allah have mercy on him) tried to explain the importance and depth of this matter by saying: "We are the community of the isnad." In addition, Muhammad Hamidullah treated this subject at length in his work entitled "Muhtasar Hadis Tarihi" (A Concise History of Hadith) and examined the matter in a scholarly way together with its evidence.

We take the information about the Prophet, in order of importance, first from the Noble Qur'an, the first revelation, then from the noble hadiths, from the science of sira, from the books of tabaqat that tell of the Prophet and his Companions, and from the studies of Islamic history.

These studies brought a method and set a procedure, and caused the Muslims to stay away from superstitions, false beliefs, mythology and legends concerning the life of the Prophet. The Prophet, this exalted example of morals, lived a natural and real life, with its bitterness and its sweetness, its happiness and its sorrow, which people can walk after and follow. The life of the Prophet has been treated and examined from beginning to end in the social, economic, political and legal sense. The Prophet was never a mythological and legendary personality with no counterpart in life. Likewise, the superstitions and false beliefs current in that period had no place in the life of the Prophet. Indeed, he struggled against them throughout his life as a prophet and spent all his effort to remove these mistaken understandings.

We stated that the prophets were sent to deliver the divine message to humanity, to convey it, to purify people and to teach them their religion. Of course a prophet who does all these things is at the same time in the position of being taken as an example and a model by people. He is to be taken as an example so that the religious practices may be learned and may be lived in human life. For this reason Almighty Allah sent to people prophets from among human beings like themselves. He did not send a spiritual being, a being of light or any other being apart from the human. If such a thing had happened, people would not have followed that prophet and would not have gone after him.

The Book of Allah and the hadiths of the Prophet have determined the fundamentals of living Islam as a religion in our life by taking the Prophet as an example in every field of life: in worship, in conveying the message and calling to it, and in the social, economic, political, legal and military fields. The principles by which we, as Muslim individuals and as a Muslim society, are to apply in our practice the way of the Prophet of exalted character have been established in this way. It has thus become clear that it is possible to live by the Sunna, which is the practice of his application of the religion, and by the science of sira.

The last prophet's administration of the state in the ten years of the Medina period and the fundamentals he laid down in this matter, his relations with his people as head of state and his international relations, and the way he carried out the practice and policy of living together in peace and security with very different beliefs and different ethnic groups, formed the foundation stones of the civilisation of Islam.

Likewise, as a commander and commander-in-chief, he laid down and himself applied very important fundamentals concerning how he trained and directed his soldiers, how he raised officers and generals, how he commanded the battles, how, by taking part in many battles in person, he ensured that the battles took place within humane limits, and concerning battles in which about 400 deaths occurred in a period of ten years. He taught both the Muslims and humanity, by applying it in person, that the aim in war is to cure the one addressed and that what is essential is not war but peace. He forbade the killing of children, women and men of religion in war, and with his fine words and orders he prevented the destruction of the environment.

Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) lived every dimension of life to the full as a human being. His conduct and his outlook on life as a husband, a father, a grandfather, a master and a civilised human being have reached us by means of these hadiths.

Why have we mentioned all these practices in summary? This umma, with its population of about two billion, has for 15 centuries been following the way of the Prophet, who had such practices, which have come down to our day, and who left a civilisation.

For a prophet whose life, conduct and words are so clearly before us, the saying "The Prophet is only a conveyer; with conveying the religion his duty ended, and he has no other duty", which has been invented especially by the enemies of Islam, is not compatible with the reality of prophethood.

It must be stated that the mentality which tries to belittle the noble Companions, who lived the civilisation of Islam and spread it to three continents, by producing slanders, suspicions and calumnies about them, is also an enemy of Islam. For they are the first generation, who delivered both the Book of Allah and the practices of the Prophet. The act of creating a negative perception of them is aimed at placing both the Book of Allah and the sunnas of the Prophet under suspicion and putting them out of use. And this is a project that will do away with the ancient traditions of the Muslims, leave them without culture and without support, and so cause them to be scattered and to move away from the essence of the religion.

To take one or a few hadiths of the Prophet and, starting from these, to create suspicions about all the hadiths, and for this reason not to accept the hadiths in any way as legal evidence, means to move away from the spirit of Islam. It is not possible to speak about the Islamic sciences without knowing the scholarly fundamentals and the rules of method, or while holding them of no account.

When speaking on this subject, not to mention the projects of the West and of orientalism would be to put a barrier in front of a good understanding of the matter. Orientalism formed the scientific pillar of the West's activity of colonising the Islamic world, which began in the 18th century. By passing on to the colonisers the information about the Islamic world and the East, the orientalists made it possible for it to be exploited for long years. By researching the cultural, political, economic, legal, scientific and social dimensions of the matter with scientific methods, they made colonialism lasting.

In recent times, for more than two centuries, the colonisers have deepened the differences among the Muslims and made them turn into division; they have prepared the ground for the Muslims to split into group after group, for the differences of thought to increase and for hatred and anger to arise. Of course, do the Muslims who fall for this game bear no guilt at all? Certainly their falling for this game by not using their reason, and so breaking up the unity and oneness of the Muslims, is something that entails sin. We cannot save ourselves by saying that others are doing this to us.

In Turkey, discussions of this kind began for the most part after the 1970s. The matters that were discussed at that time in magazines and later in newspapers gave their place to radio and television after the 1990s. After the 2000s, while the radio and television side continued, with the internet revolution this shifted to social media. As a result it has caused a pollution of information in minds and the mixing of the true with the false.

Our proposal is that scholarly matters of this kind should be discussed by scholars and thinkers in special scholarly settings, that the evidence should be set out, and that what reaches the media should be the acceptance of the most correct view. These may be, and may be set up as, symposiums, panels or special settings for scholarly discussion.

The reckless debates held in front of the masses drive people who do not know their religion to despair and affect their view of religion negatively. To ensure that this takes shape in a correct way is a great duty that falls to the scholars. And to prepare this setting for our men of knowledge and thought falls to the Muslims who have means, as the third party. The property and the money we spend in this matter will be directed wholly towards the pleasure of Allah. From the point of view of the future of the Muslims, this is as important as the good deeds we do for the poor and the orphans. Indeed, when the occasion arises, it comes before them.

Only thanks to these righteous works can we attain and live the reality of the moderate umma. The way of extremes, of excess and neglect, of ignorance and division can be closed in this way. Without knowledge and without acting in a scholarly way it is not possible to stand against ignorance and colonialism.
