Marriage Is Worship

May 18, 2017About 15 min read

Translated from the Turkish original.Read the original

Contents (3 headings)
  1. The Responsibilities of Society
  2. The Benefits of the Worship of Marriage for Humanity
  3. Can This Worship Be Abandoned?

The smallest structure of societies is made up of the family. And every family comes into being through marriage. For this reason marriage takes on a primary duty in the continuation of the human race.

Islam, as a religion, has given great importance to marriage and has laid down basic and lasting fundamentals in this matter. It has placed duties on both men and women and has held the whole of society responsible in this respect. Almighty Allah has said in a noble verse: "Marry off the single among you. If they are poor, Allah will enrich them out of His bounty and generosity. Allah is All-Encompassing, All-Knowing."1 The command "marry them off" addresses first of all the mother and father and the family, and after that it is addressed to the whole of society.

When a child who grows up and is raised within a family reaches the age of marriage, the family takes steps for him or her to form a new family too. The family, in the first place, fulfils all the obligations that fall to it for this marriage to take place. Using all its strength, it makes an effort for a healthy and good family home to be formed. If the family's means are not sufficient in the matter of marriage, then the close circle and society step in.

The Responsibilities of Society

How will society carry out its duty towards the individual who is to marry? How will it marry him off while protecting his honour and dignity? What kind of path will it follow in winning him for society? How will it bring together two young people who are suited to each other? What kind of conditions do these young people who marry and set up a home need in order to continue their marriage? Questions of this kind can go on and on. Here we want to speak of some basic principles so that society may fulfil this task in the finest way.

Society must turn its attention to this task with all the institutions and organisations that represent it and receive their salaries from it, with its municipality, its district governorship, its governorship, its parliament and its government. To deal with this is an unavoidable necessity from the point of view of the future of society.

Parliament should make marriage easier by passing laws, should remove the obstacles in front of marriage and should lower the minimum age of marriage to 16. Today the practice of the age of 16 is secured by law in England. This does not mean that our children who have not reached maturity for marriage, who do not carry the responsibility of marriage and who do not know the world must marry at this age. Indeed, we are of the conviction that today a young person of ours who has finished university, although he is 22 years old, should not marry, because he does not have the sense of responsibility to shoulder the burden of marriage. We are of the opinion that such a young person should marry only when he has received education on marriage and has come to a state in which he can carry responsibility.

Taking into account that, according to UN data, 99% of the people of our country are Muslim, regulations in keeping with the rights and the law of the Muslims should be brought in concerning marriage. Laws, statutes, circulars and regulations that damage the law of the Muslims and hold them of no account should be repealed.

The government should apply the fundamentals foreseen in the laws with its programmes of education and teaching and with its aid to families, should put facilitating measures into force, should take the measures that will make young people ready for marriage and motivate them towards it, and should ensure that improving programmes for this are passed on to society by way of the press and the media. It should increase the work directed to this in its education policies, and speed should be given to the educational work aimed at bringing both our boys and our girls to a position in which they can be ready for marriage.

The bad customs, traditions and usages that make marriage difficult and delay it should be reviewed again, and work should be done to remove them and to have good customs and conduct settle in their place. In this matter a duty falls to everyone, woman and man, old and young, poor and rich.

We do not find it right that young people who are to marry should be married, or should be made to marry, in "mass wedding" or "mass marriage contract" ceremonies, as some municipalities do today. When a kindness is to be done to a person, kindnesses should be done in a way that protects his honour and dignity. For example, direct financial help can be given to the man or the woman who is to marry. And this should not be announced to the world by way of the press and the media. The religion of Islam has assessed giving in secret as the more virtuous conduct even when giving charity.

The treasury should set aside an allocation in the budget especially for young people who are to marry. There should be a budget special to them, which only these young people can use.

Investments should be made that will ward off the negative effects brought by modernity and the Western type of way of life, work on the family should be organised, support should be given to the civil organisations that will support the family, and individuals should be helped in this matter in the first degree.

For example, the Western type of way of life does not look warmly on marriage. The statistics show that almost half of those who marry in Europe get divorced. In some European countries this rate even rises to as much as 60 and 70%.

The desire for and the practice of "living together" without entering into a marriage contract is increasing day by day. The number of women and men who do not take, and do not want to take, the responsibility of marriage and the marriage contract is also constantly growing. Although the age of marriage in England is 16 by law, in general the average age of marriage is constantly rising and climbing to the ages of 30 to 40.

Women do not want to have children and to enter into such an obligation. Instead of a family, a warm home and children, animals such as pet cats, tortoises, birds, crocodiles, tigers and lions, and more often all kinds of breeds of dogs, are preferred.

To the couples who do marry, too, the nuclear family structure is proposed, and the narrow family made up of mother and father and, with them, one or at most two children is encouraged.

Although the age of marriage in England is 16 by law, the Westernised way of thinking regards marrying at those ages in our societies as early, and by constantly making ugly propaganda it tries to arouse the image that women and men are somehow being deceived. By saying words such as "Can there be marriage at this age? They are still children", "What a pity, they have married at this age", "They have wasted their lives", "They married off the child before the child had lived its life", they cloud minds and make marriage appear unpleasant. So much so that, under the influence of this propaganda, our daughters who have reached the ages of 22 to 25 regard marrying even at this age as very early and do not feel themselves ready for marriage. Our sons at the ages of 25 to 30 also regard this age as early and do not want to marry. At this moment thousands of our young people, girls and boys, keep wandering about as single people although they are over the age of 30. This increase in the proportion of single people and the delaying of the age of marriage only make the work of the centres of evil and wickedness easier.

But in the West, marriage is constantly encouraged, and legal regulations in favour of marriage are constantly being made in this matter. Indeed, in recent times they have been setting up new initiatives and bodies in order to revive the institution of marriage.

A civilisation based purely on matter makes people the servants and slaves of matter.

One of the requirements of the Western type of way of life is also flirting. So much so that flirting has almost become a normal state in the life of our young people. Those who do not flirt are even blamed, attempts are made to belittle them, and, going still further, they are subjected to ugly insults. Today this situation has been brought down to such levels that relationships of this kind have begun to be regarded as normal even among primary and middle school pupils, and girlfriends or boyfriends have begun to be changed like changing socks. In high school and at university, flirtations of this kind continue in a measureless, boundless and ugly way. These are not done for lofty purposes such as marrying, setting up a home and having a family and children. They are carried out only for the purpose of amusing the heart, passing a pleasant time and making use of one another. Thus the moral level falls lower day by day, and the number of young people who are irresponsible and think that life consists of play and amusement increases still more. In the end, degeneration in every field wraps around society like an octopus.

A civilisation based purely on matter makes people the servants and slaves of matter. Thus money, power, rank and position are turned into idols, and people are brought down to a position in which they now value these. When the aim is only to earn money and to take pleasure from life, the purpose of life changes too. Everyone, woman and man, works with great ambition, family life is not lived in the full sense, and homes are used as hotels and restaurants. In the end, as an esteemed writer of ours, Cihan Aktaş, stated in the book she wrote, "Modernizmin Evsizliği" (The Homelessness of Modernism), a society without house or home comes into being. The working woman is rapidly moving away from the role of being 'the female bird that builds the nest'. Thus the dynamics that would keep the institution of the family standing are disappearing.

Against negative attitudes of this kind, then, it is necessary to take measures that will keep society standing and alive and will prevent it from coming under the influence of such tendencies.

As can be seen, with the command that occurs in the verse above, the family and society are held under the obligation to marry off their unmarried children. This is at the same time an act of worship, and the spending connected with it is charity and spending in the way of Allah. In this way the individuals in society who have reached the age of marriage are prevented from remaining single.

Almighty Allah has said:

"Marry those women who are lawful for you."2

The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) says, addressing the young:

"O community of young people! If you have the means, marry. If you do not have the means, then fast. For it is a shield; it breaks desire."3

In a hadith narrated by al-Bayhaqi and al-Tabarani, the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

"Whoever has the means to marry and does not marry is not one of mine."

And in another hadith he said:

"Marry and multiply. On the Day of Resurrection I shall take pride in the great number of my umma."

Again, with the aim of making marriage easy, the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "The best marriage is the easiest one."

The Benefits of the Worship of Marriage for Humanity

Before everything else, marriage makes it possible to set up a warm home.

It prevents a person from becoming lonely; woman and man together help each other in their home for many years.

It brings about the continuation of the human race in a legitimate way, in accordance with the law.

It prevents the spread of immorality among people.

It makes it possible to educate and bring up one's children in a fine way in the setting of the family.

The bonds of kinship formed by marriage do not leave a person alone and give him a strong psychology.

The bonds of kinship formed by marriage do not leave a person alone and give him a strong psychology.

Through the bonds of kinship a consciousness of society is formed, and this consciousness keeps a person standing.

By marriage a person meets the need to love and to be loved that exists in human psychology.

He has children legitimately and in accordance with the law, and experiences the love of children in the family.

With the family, which is formed as the smallest structure of society, neighbourly relations and social relations increase trust, peace and tranquillity in society.

The person who marries has protected half of his religion.

By means of marriage a person meets sexuality, which is a need of human nature, in a legitimate way.

With marriage the marriage contract is made and a legal bond is established, and all relations take place within the framework of the law.

Marriage causes relationships that are without limits and recognise neither rule nor law to decrease or to end.

Marriage teaches a person that woman and man are not enemies of each other but a structure in which each supports the other and completes what the other lacks.

Can This Worship Be Abandoned?

Can we really abandon an act of worship that takes on such an important, valuable and exalted duty? Do we have the right to do such a thing? Can we both say "I am a Muslim" and not marry?

We want to answer these questions by mentioning an event that took place in the Age of Happiness. Some Companions have a conversation among themselves of the following kind.

One of them says: "I do not sleep at night; I stand until morning and pray."

The other says: "I fast every day."

And another says: "I will not marry any woman." All three of these Companions do these acts with an innocent intention, in order to come closer to Allah and for their worship and obedience to increase.

When these words of theirs are conveyed to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), he becomes very angry and says his historic words, which are to this effect:

"I am the Messenger of Allah; I am closer to Allah than you. At night I both sleep and pray. In the daytime I both fast and eat. And I marry women. Whoever turns away from my Sunna is not one of mine."4

Thus the last prophet draws his way openly and with definite lines. The way of the Prophet… The Sunna of the Prophet… The law that Allah created… Those who leave this way leave the way of Islam and abandon the way of the Prophet. They abandon human nature, their own selves.. They flee from responsibility.. They threaten the safety of the generations..

Perhaps, too, this abandoning of the Sunna will make evil become widespread and will cause lineages to be mixed with one another. By tearing down the family home, it will drag society into strife and corruption. So the conduct that would spoil this order that has been set out is rejected severely. He lays down a measured, balanced order of life and also wants it to be observed.

Indeed, for an individual who is in danger of falling into what is forbidden, marriage rises to the position of an obligation (fard). The person who does not perform this obligation although he has the means becomes a sinner. One sin calls another, and thus disasters follow one after another.

Yes, in the end, must we not follow his way as the umma of the last prophet? Is his Sunna not our way? Our leaving him will of course be our disaster, as individuals and as a society.

When marrying, too, it is necessary to prefer the one who is religious. A woman is married for her beauty, her lineage, her property and wealth and her religiousness. But the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) advised us to prefer the one who is religious. He said: "Take the woman who is religious."5

Almighty Allah, too, has stated clearly in the following verse which woman a Muslim should prefer:

"Do not marry polytheist women until they believe. A believing slave woman is better than a polytheist woman, even if she pleases you. And do not give your women in marriage to polytheist men until they believe. A believing slave is better than a (free) polytheist, even if he pleases you. They call (you) to the Fire; but Allah calls, by His leave, to Paradise and forgiveness. Thus Allah makes His verses clear to people so that they may reflect and take heed."6

May our Lord not separate us from the way of the last Messenger. May He make us among His servants who act according to his authentic Sunna. May He grant us to set up a Muslim family home, and grant those who set up this family to live it as Muslims. May He grant all of us righteous and good children. Amen
