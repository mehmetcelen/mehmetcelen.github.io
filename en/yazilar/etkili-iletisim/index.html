Effective Communication

13 May 2017, Niamey

The human being is a social creature, the only one among all creatures that speaks and reaches understanding by speaking. What makes the human being the most valuable of creatures is that the fruits of using the mind are expressed through speech. The verse says:

"The diversity of your tongues and your colours is among the signs of Allah."1

Thus one of the most important proofs of our existence in nature is language. It is the clearest sign that a human being cannot live alone. For this reason the human being is a social creature, and communication is a requirement of our being social creatures. A saying goes: "People come to terms by talking, animals by sniffing one another." So the basis of understanding between people rests on speech. Of course this speech too has its methods and principles.

How should we be when we speak and communicate with other people? Communication is a reality that forms the foundation of society. It brings about the cohesion, solidarity and unity of society. The order of the organisations, institutions and administrative structures that exist in society is realised through communication. These may be thought of as families, states, companies, organisations, NGOs and all organised bodies.

Communication has a quality that determines the behaviour and the image of individuals and groups. People value you according to your skill, technique and ability in communicating. A saying that expresses this goes: "People are welcomed for their appearance and seen off for their knowledge." Dress, behaviour and manners are a part of a person's non-verbal communication. That is, physical appearance has an important place in social psychology. But it does not determine everything. A person may wear the costume of a sultan and very valuable clothes. At first sight these may appeal to those he meets. But if your words, your conduct and your way of relating once you have been welcomed do not match the outward appearance and prove it empty, people begin to look at you differently. It is at this point that the foundation of communication appears.

Social harmony

Communication is the basic element of social harmony. It is communication that brings about the cohesion of people, groups and societies. This also has a quality that differs and may change within social processes. In this respect communication is an important point in human life and an inseparable element of it.

The aim in communication

The aim in communication is to solve our problems in the proper way, to be able to carry on our lives well, and to meet our need for one another. The most effective quality that makes these possible is communication.

What, then, are the basic features of communication? What words and conduct are needed for communication to run soundly? Let us set out the explanation point by point.

Knowing oneself

The most important way to communicate effectively is for a person to know himself. When a person does not know himself, he cannot communicate soundly with others. As Yunus Emre says in this poem:

Knowledge is to know knowledge;

knowledge is to know yourself.

If you do not know yourself,

what then is all your reading for?

A person who is at peace with himself and knows his place and his position has no difficulty in communicating with others.

A person's knowing himself leads him to order his relations with those he deals with well. A person who is at peace with himself and knows his place and his position has no difficulty in communicating with others. For this reason there is an important saying among the people of Anatolia: "Wherever you are, know your place and your home well." If a person knows his own qualities, his shortcomings and faults and what he has in excess, he acts accordingly. This in turn lets him make his way towards good positions.

Being able to go beyond oneself

A person needs in every case to be able to express himself correctly and to go beyond himself. When we draw limits and circles around ourselves and multiply our red lines, communication weakens and comes to a standstill. In relations with people there must be the quality of acting as a gentleman and of opening up.

Listening to the other person actively and attentively

To understand others well and to perceive them correctly, one must listen attentively and actively. For example, if a person turns his face away while the other is speaking, is busy with his telephone or occupied with other things, it is not possible to communicate with him. Two or three instances of such behaviour cause relations to be cut off. It also causes hurt feelings, and hurt feelings are truly hard to mend.

Empathy with the other person

Empathy is putting oneself in the other person's place. It is taking an approach that answers the question: how would I think if I were in his place? The power of communication grows to the extent that empathy is established with the other person. Establishing empathy means being tolerant in relations, in speech and in communication. Approaching events positively and keeping away from prejudice shape empathy. Being prejudiced means drawing lines for ourselves and keeping ourselves within certain limits. This too hinders communication. In this connection the following noble hadith is full of meaning:

"None of you truly believes until he wishes for his believing brother what he wishes for himself."2

Being open to criticism

One of the most effective sides of communication is being open to the criticism one receives. This has a very important place in human life, and especially in the life of a Muslim. Criticism appears in many places in the Noble Qur'an as "enjoining what is right and forbidding what is wrong" (al-amr bi'l-ma'ruf wa'l-nahy 'an al-munkar).3 Enjoining what is right means "making the good known, conveying it, spreading it and, where the occasion calls for it, commanding it". Forbidding what is wrong means "preventing evil and keeping people away from foul and ugly deeds".

For this reason it is said: "To criticise is an act of worship." The expression "to criticise" is very wrongly understood among the people of Anatolia. When you criticise someone, it is understood as though you were humiliating or insulting him. In reality it has nothing to do with that. Criticism is to tell the other person, according to the situation, both his right and good side and, in a good manner, what is wrong and bad. Of course, measuring the dose of criticism well is an important point in communication. Humiliating and insulting language damages communication. We call this destructive criticism. What is preferred here is constructive criticism. To express what one has to say well and gracefully is to be constructive. But this perception has been engraved on minds so wrongly that even when a thing is said well and constructively it is taken negatively. There is a perception as though "I am being insulted and humiliated in front of people". We want to state that this perception and this way of approaching the matter are not right. We say: "He who does not tell me my fault in a good manner is not my friend." A person who does not tell me my fault wants me to continue in my fault. Our approach is that such a person cannot be our friend.

Body language in communication

In communication not only words and speech but body language too is important. The eyes, the tongue, the form of address, the level of the voice, the manner of behaving, gestures and facial expressions are all important. For example, to carry on an ordinary conversation in a tone of voice as though one were about to strike damages and harms communication. Speaking to the other person by shouting breaks communication and blocks it. Our messages may be verbal, non-verbal or written. The verbal is the use of spoken language, and the non-verbal is the use of body language and sign language. Thus the messages given to others are determined by perceptions. That is why the perception of the other side is very important, and one must speak with attention to the other person's perception as well. That is, when we express our words we must not say them in a form and manner that could cause the other person to perceive them wrongly.

In addition, in perception one must take into account the other person's physiological and psychological state, his experiences, his cultural values and his emotional position. For example, to laugh and guffaw and make amusing remarks in front of someone who is sad because a painful event has befallen him is to fail to see the pain that is there. This too causes a perception to form in a bad direction. It is also useful to know how one should approach events from the point of view of environmental factors. If we do not know them, we must first of all learn these factors. Where we cannot learn them, we must postpone communicating.

Communication at every time and in every place

Communication takes place everywhere and at all times, and it is done with the person. The most important factor in being effective and strong in one's surroundings is good communication. Meanings must be shared by the parties. The words must be understood in the same way on both sides so that communication can be established correctly. This holds at all times.

The basic principle in this matter is this hadith of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him): "The believer is the one from whose hand and tongue others are safe."4 Again, in another noble hadith, to the question:

"Which of the Muslims is the most excellent?"

he replied:

"The one from whose hand and tongue the Muslims are safe."5

To be safe means that people are in security, peace and trust. The believer is a person who can be trusted with his hand and with his tongue. The word hand here expresses behaviour, the body and sign language. It takes in all the organs of our body. Thus through our conduct and our body language we must provide security and make people trust us. As can be seen, the last Prophet stated that the hand and the tongue play a great part in communication. It is not possible to communicate with people who are not trusted, whose speech does harm and who damage relations with their hands and their bodies. Just as communication cannot be established with them, there can be no question of continuing what exists either. For the continuation of communication depends on the continuation of trust, peace and tranquillity. As long as this peace is not secured by your tongue and your body language, this communication will not exist.

Giving the greeting of peace and spreading it

One of the most important elements of communication is to give the greeting of peace (salam) and to spread it. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

"Greet everyone, those you know and those you do not know."6 "Spread the greeting of peace among yourselves."7 And the verse says:

"Respond to a greeting in the best manner."8

The greeting is the sign of peace, well-being and security. It is the road of peace that goes from heart to heart. Through the greeting, trust is established between individuals and in society. For example, a person says: I give the greeting, and he does not return my greeting. How will the one who gives the greeting approach the one who does not return it, and how will he communicate with him? Even if that person is a Muslim, it makes no difference. My being a Muslim, my performing the prayer, my fasting and my other acts of worship are between Almighty Allah and myself. But good social relations too are worship. For the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Religion is how one deals with others." And the verse says: "Live with them in kindness."9 What is meant here is relations with people: relations with other people, with Muslims, with non-Muslims and with everyone. That is why the Prophet's counsel "Spread the greeting" is very important. When the greeting is given, one is in fact also saying hello. The word salam carries the meaning of peace. So you are spreading peace, and you are saying: I want to live in peace with you, and I am at peace with myself too. In the end the foundation of trust in society is laid.

Good character

But this much is certain: people of good character are the people whose communication is the best and the finest.

One of the most basic features of communication is good character. Some may say: where did this come from? But this much is certain: people of good character are the people whose communication is the best and the finest. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) says:

"The one whose character is the best is the one whose faith is the most complete."10

"The best of you is the one of you whose character is the best."11

"Islam is good character."12

When we look at these hadiths, a link is made between character and faith and Islam. Yet character is a matter that concerns human behaviour. In spite of that, a direct link is made with creed, belief and religion. Thus the relations of people of good character take on flesh and bone with faith and appear as a whole. These expressions show how important this is for religion.

As can be seen, a person's individual behaviour is reflected, in sum, as social behaviour. Thus the web of social relations is built and the bond of brotherhood is lived. For example, in our day there can be people who say: "I can perform the prayer and still do evil." In a capitalist system especially, such things happen a great deal. To defend the injustice he has done, a person says: "Sir, this is trade, it can happen." Yet even in trade one must act honestly. The merchant will not give short weight, will not lie, will not deceive people and will not sell bad goods as good. When all these wrongs are present, to give this the name of trade is of course not acceptable.

Here we want to give examples about good character from the prophets. The verse says:

"Had you been harsh and hard of heart, they would have scattered from around you."13

One must reflect a great deal on this verse. He is both one of the prophets of firm resolve (ulu'l-'azm), a very great one, and the last Prophet. The verse says directly "you" and "if you act like this". He is at the same time a prophet who is the leader of all Muslims; a human being supported at all times by Allah; a person to whom revelation came and who had superior qualities that set him apart from other people. In spite of all this, the warning that is given is very important: "They would have scattered from around you." That is: when you behave like this, your prophethood has no meaning. Your prophethood cannot save you. You must secure this with your tongue and with your conduct as well. And indeed the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) used fine language throughout his life and realised communication at the highest level. He always acted in this way also in order to convey the religion of Allah to people. As can be seen, it is made clear that even if one is a prophet, and even if one has the Book of Allah in one's hand, no one will remain at one's side when ugly and coarse behaviour is shown. At the same time there is a message here for the community of the Prophet too. It is being said: you too, O Muslims, must be like this. You must not fall into hard-hearted, coarse, harsh and ugly behaviour.

Another example is Moses (peace be upon him). Although he had received every kind of help from Almighty Allah, he asks that his brother Aaron (peace be upon him) be given to him as a helper. Here, whether Moses (peace be upon him) considered himself sufficient or not, in the end he asks for a helper for himself. "Go, both of you, and speak to Pharaoh with gentle words. Perhaps he will reflect and take heed, or fear."14 Pharaoh was the greatest tyrant of the society of that day and a powerful head of state who held power in his hands. Although he was very tyrannical and unjust, the instruction is given: "Go to him and speak gentle words." We have a proverb: "A sweet tongue draws the snake out of its hole." A fine and sweet word can draw even a harmful animal such as a snake out of its hole. Even if the person addressed is a tyrant, even if he is a very bad person, one must speak soft and sweet words to him.

Patience and forbearance

Being patient and forbearing is also an element that keeps communication going. Sometimes negative situations, positions that are not right and events we dislike may occur. In such a case acts in the form of holding back may take place. We call this patience. "Patience is a light."15 It lights the way of the believer and brings him out of darkness. At the same time patience is a great act of worship.

For example, when Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) was slandered, the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) did not speak with people for about a month. He went to the prayer and led it, but a great offence such as slander was there in the open. There was nothing to prove the matter either. That being so, he withdrew into seclusion for a month. If one notices, he was both the Prophet and the head of state and also the greatest leader of that region. In spite of all this, he tried to keep away from people. He removed himself from communication and interaction. There is a similar situation with the prophet Zachariah (peace be upon him). In the face of the slanders made he kept a "fast of not speaking". The meaning of this is to draw oneself back. Otherwise the prophet was still among the people, leading the prayer and giving his counsel, but he was not active as on other days. These too are points to which attention must be paid in communication.

Doing good

Doing good is also one of the basic features of communication. Doing good should not be thought of only as giving goods and material help. It has a spiritual side too. To show a smiling face is a kindness. To speak well is a kindness. To say welcome when someone comes to visit you is a kindness. When someone comes, to show him a seat and say "please" rather than standing there rudely is also a kindness. On the subject of these kindnesses the verse says:

"If you do good, you will see that the one who was your enemy has become a warm friend."16

With someone who has cut off relations and communication with you, the good you do to him restores communication and can build relations of friendship. For this reason the verses say:

"Do good."17

"Surely Allah commands justice and the doing of good."18

"Surely Allah loves those who do good."19 Expressions of this kind occur often in the Qur'an. Such people are people who are kneaded with goodness throughout their lives. Good is the opposite of evil and kindness the contrary of wickedness. To keep away from evil and to be the companion of goodness will make communication take place at the highest level.

Understanding the other person's state of mind

It is very important to understand the state of mind of the person before us and to act accordingly. We have to carry on our relations taking into account moments of joy and sorrow and the right moment for a thing. When we speak of understanding a person's state of mind, in our tradition this is referred to as the rights and duties of one believer towards another.

Conduct such as attending the funeral of one's brother and offering condolences, visiting him when he falls ill, and joining him when he invites one to his wedding opens the door of communication. These are the relations that bring about communication and keep it going. When these relations are not kept up, the other side will come once, will come twice, and after that will come no more. He will come neither to your funeral, nor in your illness, nor to your wedding. In this way communication will have been destroyed.

Understanding the finer points

Understanding the finer points is also a very important principle in communication. It is to be of a sensitive spirit and to be able to notice the nuances in the words and conduct of those we deal with. A practical example from the working environment may be given. For example, a lower level of management issues a message like this: "By the decision of our head office a photograph will be taken." This message does not grant those in the lower unit the right to use their own initiative. The message here must be understood very well. It is not written: "Come along, friends, you have to come to have the photograph taken" or "Attendance is compulsory." The decision of the head office is conveyed in a more courteous expression. Another example: when inviting people to a cultural event, if it is said "I particularly ask you to come", the meaning of this is that attendance is compulsory. For a request from a higher office to the lower units is an order.

Acting diplomatically

Diplomacy is a method used more in relations between countries and in international relations. In this respect it lies in the field of political science. Nevertheless it holds an important place in every area of our lives. It is to know the traditions, usages, customs and different cultures of the surroundings in which we find ourselves and to develop a way of behaving accordingly.

For example, when a person is going to marry, he needs to know the culture and traditions of those he is dealing with, and to order and develop his relations accordingly. Of course he will point out to the other party a thing that is wrong according to his own beliefs and values, and will courteously ask that it not be done. Or he will let it be known that it should not be asked of him.

Listening to one side only

To listen to one side only of events that have passed between two or more people and then to reach a decision and a judgment is a great mistake. Even if the decision reached happens to be right, the manner is wrong, because it is contrary to the rules of acting justly. This too causes relations with the person concerned to be damaged and communication to be harmed. In the language of law this is called "execution without trial".

If there is a case, the parties to it must by all means be heard. Only in this way is it possible to understand the reality of the matter. Then one is counted as having acted justly in procedure as well. In this way people's outlook becomes more positive, and communication and relations continue in a healthy way.

Accusation and suspicion

Accusation is to regard the other person as guilty, to lay guilt upon him and to charge him without listening to him and without investigating the truth of the matter.

Suspicion (zann) is to comment on a matter in a state of doubt, without having certain knowledge about it. Its existence or non-existence may or may not be within the bounds of possibility. To speak about such a matter as though it had happened is suspicion itself.

To accuse a person of something that is not in him, or to present it as though it were, damages relations. It cuts off the communication that exists between people. For that reason one must not enter into such negative attitudes without knowing the truth of a matter.

"Yet they have no knowledge of this; they follow nothing but conjecture, and conjecture can never take the place of the truth."20

"O you who believe, avoid much suspicion, for some suspicion is a sin. And do not pry into one another's hidden affairs."21

"Beware of suspicion, for suspicion is the most untruthful of speech."22

In conclusion, communication is the common side of humanity, which must exist in every field. Societies in which communication is strong and positive are healthy societies. The element that secures the permanence of societies and lets them walk with confidence into the future is effective communication. With this article we have tried, if only a little, to explain the points to which attention must be paid.
