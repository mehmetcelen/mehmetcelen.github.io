A- The Phenomenon of Divorce in Turkey

In order to determine the course of the phenomenon of divorce and to be able to reach a sound conclusion, we need to observe the rates of divorce.

The divorce statistics of the countries of the world by year, published by Eurostat, have been given. The highest rate of divorce was established for America, one of the developed countries of the world, as 4.2 per thousand for the year 1999. In Japan, another developed country, the rate of divorce is seen to have been 1.6 per thousand in 1996.

Again according to the Eurostat data, when the rates of divorce in some countries and in Turkey are looked at, it is seen that our country, compared with the other countries, is one of the countries with the lowest rate of divorce. While the crude divorce rate in the countries of the European Union is 2.1 per thousand, it is noteworthy that in Turkey the crude divorce rate in 2007, at 1.3 per thousand, was lower than in many countries of the world. The countries with the highest rate of divorce are, with the USA at their head, Lithuania and Latvia.

According to Yörükoğlu (1997), the countries of North America, and especially the USA, have always led in divorce. For example, while in 1915 one in every ten marriages ended in divorce, in 1974 one in every 2.3 marriages ended in divorce. In 1973 it was established that there were 8.2 marriages and 5.7 divorces per thousand persons. Only 44 per cent of the population is made up of unbroken households.

One seventh of all children are the children of divorced parents.

Researchers state that in the last 30 years there has been a dramatic increase in the rates of divorce in the world, and that, for example, in America at least half of new and first marriages result in divorce. That the rate of divorce, which began to show an increase in America in the 1960s and 1970s, reached its highest levels in the 1980s (Sayar, 2007) runs parallel to the family policies applied in the developed countries and to the evolution the family has undergone. Indeed in the developed Western countries the break-up of the family, divorce, single parenthood, the weakening of relations within the family, relations of kinship and neighbourliness coming to the point of breaking, the spread of the use of addictive substances and the pathologies to which all these give rise in the life of the individual, the family and society draw attention as the basic problems concerning the family (Özek, 2007).

The situation in Europe is no different from this. Almost one in every two marriages results in divorce. According to the data announced by the statistical institute of Belgium, two out of every three marriages in its own country will result in divorce. As can be seen, the rate of divorce in the West has climbed to levels of 70 %.2

When it is taken into consideration that in the developed countries marriages result in divorce under the influence of factors such as tendencies towards individualisation, rising prosperity, excessive consumption and the economic independence of the spouses, there is a need, in order to prevent divorce from becoming an ever-growing problem in our country too, for a database to be formed by carrying out studies that set out the basic causes leading to divorce and the frame of the factors affecting these causes.3

In Turkey the rates, especially of the last ten years, show that divorce is running at a level that could become a social problem for our society. The rate of divorce, which showed a slow increase in the 1990s, turned between 2000 and 2010 into a regular increase and has come to threaten society.

Although this picture that emerges looks smaller than the rate in the West, if it is considered that 99 % of the population of Turkey is Muslim, we can say that these are very large figures and that the phenomenon of divorce is heading towards a very dangerous course. These figures explain, in a manner that leaves no room for doubt, that divorce is a social problem and a threat.

Infidelity/betrayal of the marriage appears before us as the most important cause of divorce. The study shows that the most important cause of divorce is not, as most of us suppose, economic causes but infidelity (24.5 %). It is followed by physical violence/beating with 17.6 %, lack of love between the spouses with 17.4 % and bad habits such as excessive alcohol and gambling with 17.3 %.20 In short, the family is dissolving because of the negative aspects brought by modern urban life and the violence that men use against their women.

The Grounds for Divorce in the Turkish Civil Code

I. Adultery

ARTICLE 161.- If one of the spouses commits adultery, the other spouse may bring an action for divorce.

The right of action lapses when six months have passed from the time the spouse entitled to the action learns of the ground for divorce, and in any case when five years have passed since the act of adultery. The party who forgives has no right of action.

II. Attempt on life, very bad or degrading treatment

ARTICLE 162.- Each of the spouses may bring an action for divorce on the ground that the other has made an attempt on his or her life, or has treated him or her very badly, or has behaved in a gravely degrading manner. The right of action lapses when six months have passed from the time the spouse entitled to the action learns of the ground for divorce, and in any case when five years have passed since this ground arose. The party who forgives has no right of action.

III. Committing a crime and leading a dishonourable life

ARTICLE 163.- If one of the spouses commits a humiliating crime or leads a dishonourable life, and for these reasons the other spouse cannot be expected to live together with him or her, this spouse may bring an action for divorce at any time.

IV. Desertion

ARTICLE 164.- Where one of the spouses deserts the other with the purpose of not fulfilling the obligations arising from the marital union, or does not return to the common home without a justified reason, if the separation has lasted at least six months and this situation is continuing, and the warning given by the judge upon request has remained without result, the deserted spouse may bring an action for divorce. The spouse who forces the other to leave the common home, or prevents the other from returning to the common home without a justified reason, is also deemed to have deserted.

V. Mental illness

Mental illness is defined in the following manner

ARTICLE 165.- If one of the spouses is mentally ill and for this reason common life becomes unbearable for the other spouse, this spouse may bring an action for divorce, on condition that it is established by the report of an official medical board that there is no possibility of the illness passing.

VI. Breakdown of the marital union

ARTICLE 166.- If the marital union has been shaken from its foundation to such a degree that the spouses cannot be expected to continue common life, each of the spouses may bring an action for divorce. In the cases stated in the paragraph above, if the fault of the plaintiff is graver, the defendant has the right to object to the action brought. Nevertheless, if this objection is in the nature of an abuse of right and no benefit worth protecting remains for the defendant and the children in the continuation of the marital union, divorce may be decreed. If the marriage has lasted at least one year, where the spouses apply together or one spouse accepts the action of the other, the marital union is deemed to have been shaken from its foundation. For a decree of divorce to be given in this case, it is a condition that the judge, having heard the parties in person, be convinced that their wills have been freely declared, and find suitable the arrangement to be accepted by the parties regarding the financial consequences of the divorce and the situation of the children. The judge, keeping in view the interests of the parties and of the children, may make the changes he considers necessary in this agreement. Where these changes are also accepted by the parties, divorce is decreed. In this case the provision that the admissions of the parties do not bind the judge is not applied. Where it has been decided to reject an action brought on any one of the grounds for divorce, and three years have passed from the date on which this decision became final, if common life has not been re-established for whatever reason, the marital union is deemed to have been shaken from its foundation and divorce is decreed upon the request of one of the spouses.

With an average rate of 95 %, the cause of incompatibility takes first place. After it come, in order, "desertion, adultery, crime and dishonourable life, attempt on life and very bad treatment".

Incompatibility is also described as "severe incompatibility" or "the breakdown of the unity of the family".

It is of course not possible for divorce, which has become a social problem in society, to have one or a few causes. This problem has been formed of complicated causes, such as social, cultural, economic, psychological and sociological causes, and has come to the stage it is at today.

As the result of our research we have come to the conviction that two main factors, the external factors and conditions of the environment, and the particular and internal factors, speed up divorces.

1- The External Factors of Divorce and the Conditions of the Environment

In our society many factors and causes that surround the individual and the family are hidden forces that trigger divorces. In Turkey incompatibility makes up the cause of divorce to a great extent, at an average rate of 95 %.

Well then, why does the incompatibility come into question of two people who, by marrying, wish to live together for a lifetime and who, at the time they marry, do not let divorce so much as cross their minds?

Why can they not live together?

Why do they not apply the law of partnership in marriage, and take the matter as far as conflict and the shaking of the bond of marriage?

Why do people who marry loving each other destroy their union and their family life with severe incompatibility?

Of course these have social, cultural, psychological, economic and even political and legal causes that are not limited to two persons alone. In the end these causes speed up divorce. The experience of all of them together, or the presence of several of them, speeds up divorce still more. We can list these factors in the following way.

a- Mosques, tekkes, zaviyes, dergâhs and cemevis,

b- The Social Services Institution,

c- Psychologists, psychiatrists, psychological counsellors, social work specialists, family therapists and family counsellors,

d- Doctors, health centres, midwives and nurses,

e- The courts,

f- The police and the gendarmerie,

g- The municipalities,

h- Education and teaching,

i- Catholic marriage,

j- Anxiety about the future, urbanisation and material means,

k- Unemployment/the hardship of economic conditions,

l- The media's incitement of divorce,

m- The laws not being arranged in a manner suited to the Anatolian style of family,

n- Feminist movements.

2- THE PARTICULAR AND INTERNAL FACTORS OF DIVORCE

Problems arising from the individual or within the family, and particular circumstances, also appear before us among the causes that affect divorce.

a- The feeling of "I", not being able to be "we",

b- The psychological and sociological factors that affect the individual,

c- The woman's desire to establish superiority in the period when the family enters a bottleneck,

d- The absence of equivalence/kafa'a between woman and man,

e- The phenomenon of living together without marriage,

f- The woman's view of the man, and the man's view of the woman, being only as a sexual object,

g- Seeing marriage as a burden and not taking risks,

h- The negative effects on the individual of the divorces around him,

i- Intolerance,

j- The excessive love of families for their children,

k- The man's desire to marry other women,

l- Money, matter and position coming to the fore in individuals.

Let us try to examine these under separate headings.

a- Mosques, tekkes, zaviyes, dergâhs and cemevis

aa. Mosques and Masjids

The mosque and the masjid, in the purpose of their foundation, have a quality that embraces the whole of life. In the period of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), in the period of the Companions after him and in the periods of the later Islamic states, they always performed this function. Mosques offered social support to society and were in the position of the centre of scholarly, intellectual and cultural education.

Today, however, mosques have been reduced to the position of places where only the prayer is performed. To a great extent, the imams and muezzins not being well equipped and social, their not concerning themselves with the troubles of the members of the congregation, and no setting for this concern being formed, have had the result that mosques cannot respond to social events.

Imams and muezzins need to be trained who have the knowledge and equipment to share the troubles of the members of the congregation of each mosque and of society, to concern themselves with their needs, to make peace in their quarrels and to bring their disputes to a solution. Perhaps today's regulations may not be suited to this, but this position can be realised by changing the regulations, making them suited to it, and also increasing the material means of these officials. In the first stage the problem can also be solved by in-service training.

In particular, preachers, women preachers and religious officials who know Shafi'i fiqh very well and are themselves Shafi'i need to be appointed to Eastern and South-eastern Anatolia, to the cities and regions where Shafi'is live in great numbers. Religious service needs to be brought to our citizens who belong to the Shafi'i school within the frame of their own school.

Furthermore, for preachers, women preachers, imams and khatibs to hold sermons, Friday addresses and religious talks in Kurdish in the regions where Kurdish is predominantly spoken, and for the commands and prohibitions of the religion to be explained so, is the conduct best suited to human nature. To address people in the language they know best is one of the indispensable principles of conveying the message and of the call. Of course the language a person knows best is the mother tongue. The difference of languages is a requirement of creation and is simply a sign among the signs of Allah.

That mosques are opened only at the times of prayer, and are kept closed outside these times on the grounds that there may be theft, prevents the solidarity, unity and mutual help that should form in the mosque. It hinders the mosque from fulfilling its essential duty.

We convey the assessment of these proposals of ours to our society and to the Presidency of Religious Affairs.

ab. Tekkes, Zaviyes and Dergâhs

Tekke: The place where the members of a Sufi order are lodged, spend their time, perform dhikr and worship, receive knowledge and lessons and hold ceremonies. In former times it is the name of the place where the men of the order gathered and held ceremonies.

Dergâh: The place or building where the dervishes gather and hold their rites. It is the name given to a large tekke.

Zaviye: It is a small tekke.

With Law no. 677 on the Closure of Tekkes, Zaviyes and Tombs and on the Prohibition and Abolition of the Office of Tomb-Keeper and of Certain Titles, which entered into force on 30 November 1925, the closure of tekkes, zaviyes and tombs was adopted and the use of some traditional titles was forbidden. This law entered into force by being published with the same number in the official gazette on 13 December 1925, and it is still in force.

As the result of tekkes, zaviyes and dergâhs having been closed by law since 1925, these places are deprived of the elements that would protect the social fabric and ensure solidarity and mutual support, unity and togetherness, and mutual help. This deprivation speeds up the dissolution in society still more and breaks the bonds that should exist.

Although today these places are in fact permitted and are open under various names, they cannot carry out their duty in the full sense because the prohibition and restrictions in the laws continue.

As the result of their not being able to carry out their duty, the absence of competent persons in these places, and their being in the hands of persons who are not qualified and do not truly know the Islamic sciences, cause the wounds and the deficiency to deepen still more. These places, which are at present free and open in fact, need to be free by law as well, in respect of their protecting the social fabric and the family.

ac. Cemevis

Cemevi: The gathering place of the Alevis.

Cemevis are the gathering place and centre of Anatolian Alevism. They are the places where the Alevis come together in these centres and where rites, social matters, mutual help and solidarity are carried out. These places must be thought of not as the counterpart of the mosque and the masjid but as being in the position of the tekkes and zaviyes.

For this reason official status needs to be given to the cemevis so that the Alevis may secure the social fabric and support among themselves and live their own thought.

b- The Social Services Institution

The social services institutions have not been able to free themselves from formal relations today. The civil-servant mentality there prevents society from being approached with love and compassion. First of all the officials who work here need to be chosen in a very special way. People need to be chosen who are merciful, well-mannered, whose love of human beings is at a high level, who fear Allah, who know serving people to be a nobility and take pleasure in it, who are generous, of good morals and of good character. It is also a condition that the existing officials be reformed through in-service training. Officials who are not reformed should be employed outside the Social Services Institution, in institutions suited to their own make-up, and should be prevented from working in these institutions.

Among the specialists who will take up duty in the Social Services Institution there should be "psychologists, psychiatrists, psychological counsellors, sociologists, social work specialists, family therapists and family counsellors, jurists and even jurists of Islamic law". Taking as the basis that 99 % of our people are Muslim, it is absolutely necessary that a jurist of Islamic law be present in these institutions. One of the greatest deficiencies of these institutions is that they turn a blind eye to this situation and characteristic.

c- Health Workers and Institutions

We can examine this subject under two main headings.

ca. Psychologists, psychiatrists, psychological counsellors, social work specialists, family therapists and family counsellors

These are the specialists and centres applied to when negative developments appear in family relations, or for the healing of a family life that has broken down. The relations of these specialists with the persons who apply to them should not be in the manner of customer and seller, but should take place in more sincere and warm settings.

These specialists should have comprehensive and sufficient knowledge not only of the psychological dimension of the matter and that of the disorder, but also of the person's religion, belief, customs and traditions. As we observe here and there today, some specialists, although they do not have sufficient knowledge of these subjects, make wrong assessments and give wrong guidance, and reduce to an impossible state positions that could probably be put right and healed.

In family counselling centres, and in the associations and foundations concerned with this subject, there need to be "psychologists, psychiatrists, psychological counsellors, sociologists, social work specialists, family therapists and family counsellors, jurists and even jurists of Islamic law", in order to be able to examine and assess the matter from many sides. Seen from this angle, it is not possible for us to say that today these centres are duly sufficient.

In our country there are not many applications to family counselling centres, and the positive effect of these centres in solving problems comes about at a rate as far behind as 23 %.22

cb. Doctors, health centres, rehabilitation, family counselling centres, midwives and nurses

That some doctors, nurses and midwives, although it is not their duty, speak words that go as far as insult, such as "Does one marry at this age?", "Does one have a child at this age?" and the like, especially to women who married at a young age, or to women who have had a child, or to married couples, awakens in those people the conviction that the marriage was made wrongly. Especially in official health institutions, in the health centre, the state hospital and the medical centres, in the fields of gynaecology, obstetrics and paediatrics, their treating their patients badly has been among the points that trigger these negative results. But the important arrangements made in these last 6 years have reduced bad behaviour of this kind to very little. We can mention this here as a positive development.

Some doctors and nurses look down on women who have a child at the ages of 19, 20, 21, insult them and speak like this:

"A child at this age? Do you not think of yourselves at all? What a pity, does one have a child at this age? Are you stupid? Come to your senses." What other words like these do they not utter. It is true that talk of this kind has decreased compared with the past, but it does not seem possible for us to say that it has ended. The rough and bad treatment of their patients who give birth at a young age also forms another side of the matter.

d- The Courts

Actions for divorce were formerly heard by the Civil Courts of First Instance, and are now heard by the Family Courts. These courts, with the decisions they give, play very important roles in the life of the family and of society.

That formal relations are in the foreground in the courts reduces mediation between quarrelling families and the act of reconciling.

Court cases that drag on for years, lasting 3 to 4 years, and delayed decisions increase the enmities, the hatred and the anger between families, and at times give rise to grave events that end in death and maiming. Justice delayed is not justice. In the end the matter does not stop at divorce alone, but causes social hatred and loathing to form.

From this angle the courts need to conclude actions for divorce more practically and in a short time. In any case most couples come to the court having already destroyed the bond of marriage between them.

The hearing in the courts of cases such as "My husband raped me." and "The father or the mother abducted my child." poses a threat to the family home, to unity and togetherness, and causes families to break up and enmities to increase.

The decisions of the enforcement offices to seize the home have also had the result that not only the debtor but the whole family is punished. They have lowered the standing of the man within the family and of the family among the neighbours and relatives, and have caused quarrels within the family to increase. Enforcement laws and regulations of this type need to change.

e- The police and the gendarmerie

In quarrels and violence within the family, the centres first applied to are the police station in the cities and the gendarmerie station in rural regions.

In order that the police and the gendarmerie may be of more help to quarrelling families, they need to receive in-service training in the field of rehabilitation and family therapy, especially in a manner that will provide social and psychological support. Or else a psychologist, a psychiatrist and a social work specialist who have specialised in this field need to be assigned to these stations. In cases of this kind, the first interventions being made by the hand of these specialists will produce sounder results.

Seen from this angle, it will be more positive for the family relations section to be looked after by these specialists or by officials who have received in-service training.

The subjects of the in-service training should first of all be these:

  • Rehabilitation
  • Social support
  • Psychological support
  • The structure of belief of society
  • The customs and traditions valid in the region
  • The ways of perceiving events
  • Approaches to the family
  • The ways of behaving towards woman and man, and so on.

f- The municipalities and the civil administrators

fa. The Municipalities

The municipalities have a very important place in our social life. For this reason very great duties fall to the municipalities in social matters. The municipalities can form and run many institutions within the frame of their own means. In fact many of our municipalities carry out duties of this kind. But the most important thing is to raise the quality of the service given there and to fill out its content.

  • Social support must be provided to families, and institutions that will provide it must be formed,
  • Rehabilitation centres must be established,
  • Family health centres must be formed,
  • In social relations the basic human rights and freedoms must be adhered to,
  • The staff who will take up duty in these centres must be chosen with great care.

I do not wish to pass on here without stating the following point. The municipalities and the official departments cut off the water, the electricity and the natural gas of families. In our opinion a family should not be punished for using basic human rights such as water, natural gas and electricity. We find practices of this kind contrary to basic human rights and freedoms. We are of the opinion that the administrators who carry out practices of this kind are committing an offence. If persons and institutions are abusing the matter, these official institutions of ours can go to the judiciary and defend their own rights there. Otherwise, if people are in this position out of need, it is the duty of society to give them social assistance. Of course society carries out this duty of its through the governments, the municipality and the social institutions attached to the governments. Governments and municipalities, of whatever party they may be formed, are obliged to fulfil their most important social duty towards society. Governments and municipalities that help only their own supporters, and practise favouritism and partisanship, are bad and unjust administrations.

It needs to be known that cutting off the water, electricity and natural gas of the house or flat in cases where the heads of families do not or cannot pay their debts increases quarrels within the family, and that the punishment is given to the whole family, and this primitive attitude needs to be abandoned. This is the duty of all the parties that have a group in the National Assembly, and they need to dwell on this matter with seriousness.

fb. The Civil Administrators

Great duties also fall to the governorships and district governorships, which conduct the civil administration, in the matter of healing social wounds.

  • Since unemployment is a great problem in our country, the unemployed must be identified and fields that will give them work must be formed, and the Employment Agency must be continually monitored and inspected so that it works more soundly.
  • They must continually monitor, report on and inspect the sound functioning of the Social Services Institution.
  • Among the citizens under their own administration there must be no family that is thrown out on the street, especially because it cannot pay its rent.
  • Social assistance and solidarity must be fulfilled in the full sense, as a requirement of being a social state.
  • Misers, the ignorant, the immoral, and types who practise favouritism, partisanship and ill-treatment must not be brought to the head of the institutions that give social service.

g- Education and teaching

Family education is a very important subject in ensuring the continuation of the family and of society. Today, from primary education to higher education, no serious education is given on this subject. The situation in the schools is teaching rather than education. Thus our children cannot receive education on this subject at a sufficient level, indeed in a manner that may be called none at all.

Especially today it has become a necessity that lessons in "family education" and "family law" be introduced in secondary education.

We propose that lessons in "family education" and "family law" be applied by the Ministry of National Education.

h- Catholic marriage

When Catholic marriage is mentioned, the first thing that comes to mind is the obligation of a man to remain married to one woman for life. He can in no way divorce her, and under whatever conditions he cannot take a second wife. For him to take a second wife legally entails a penal sanction. In any case the laws too do not permit marriage of this kind.

The civil law of Turkey was imported directly from the Swiss civil law. At first it was applied to society in the form in which it was translated, without any intervention at all. And Switzerland took its own civil law from the city of Neuchâtel, which is very strictly bound to the Catholic law of Christianity.

Although certain changes have been made to this civil law, in the main its existence continues today and it is still applied to our society. For this reason it gives a prison sentence of more than one year to the man who makes a second marriage by a contract of marriage according to Islamic law, but gives no punishment at all to the man who lives together with more than one woman. Because of this, unions without marriage are increasing.

The marriages of men who wish to take a second wife according to Islamic law end, to a great extent, in divorce. As a result great ruins and enmities come about. To examine the position of marriages of this kind is not our subject here. We only wish to state that this has an effect as a cause of divorce.

i- Anxiety about the future, urbanisation and material means

The statistics show that the rate of divorce in the metropolitan cities is considerably higher than in the other cities and the rural regions.

Urbanisation and the increase of material means reduce the responsibilities and obligations of people towards one another, make taking risks harder and needlessly increase the feeling of not being beholden to anyone.

The increase of material means has also changed the way of looking at the family and at marriage. Seeing marriage as a tangle of troubles, and defining it as a risk and as a factor that restricts freedom, is spreading. This view is the way of looking of modernity and of the West, and it is spreading more and more in our society. In the end the young turn towards not marrying, and married young couples towards divorce.

In the Ottoman period and in the period of the Republic until 1950 our society remained an agricultural society. 80 % of society lived in rural regions and 20 % in the cities. The existing proportion in Turkey began to change after the 1950s, and by today this proportion has turned into the exact opposite. At present 80 % of the population lives in the city.23

Anxiety about the future brings with it a life full of fear and worry. These worries and fears affect married life negatively. And the greatest factor that forms anxiety about the future is the worry that the material means one has will slip from one's hand.

ı- Unemployment/the hardship of economic conditions,

Unemployment is one of the very first problems of Turkey. At present the total population of Turkey24 is 73.722.988 persons (31 December 2010). The total labour force is 22.165.000 persons. (January 2010)

According to the data of April 2011 the rate of unemployment has gone down to around 12 %. Among the young this rate reaches 25 %.25 However, even though the number of the unemployed looks small in the nominal sense, in the real sense the total of the unemployed is on average around 3.5 million. And this makes up a very large number. It corresponds on average to almost 20 %.

Above all, that one in four young people is unemployed, and moreover that the hidden unemployed are too many to be made light of, increases the poverty of the people still more.

Unemployment, in the social sense, means "hunger and disaster".

The statistics made show that half of the people (46.6 %) cannot make ends meet with half of their own income.26 In a country where even those who work suffer hardship in making a living, it becomes still easier to estimate the dimension of the unrest in the family.

Unemployment, in the social sense, means "hunger and disaster". A person who does not work cannot buy bread for his home and cannot boil a hot soup in his home. It means that the married life of the man who cannot support his home, cannot provide the living of his wife and his children and cannot secure their maintenance is under threat. And this causes the problems in the family to increase, the quarrels and the noise to multiply and the wounds not to be bound up. In the end the breaking of the bonds of marriage and the ruin of homes becomes unavoidable.

Those who say, as the cause of the divorce, "our falling into hardship in making a living" are a proportion as large as 38.8 %.27

The most pressing matter for governments is to find a remedy for the problem of unemployment by increasing employment. For this problem gives birth to many social problems along with it. One of the most important causes of the increase of divorces is also the problem of unemployment. To throw the blame on persons alone means choosing the easy way out. It is not individuals but governments that will solve the problem of unemployment.

Economic hardship is a problem not only during the marriage but also in the process of divorce and after it. According to Arıkan (1992), the most important problem both of women at the stage of divorce and of divorced women is economic hardship.28

j- The media's incitement of divorce

The media, and especially the television channels, belittle family life with serials and films, incite divorce and encourage the phenomenon of living together. The newspapers, magazines and radios too, the great majority of which are in the hands of westernised minds, fulfil without fail these duties entrusted to them.

In serials and films, the bringing to the fore of the influence of characters who present a rosy life, the constant stories of deceit, and ways of life that run counter to the beliefs and customs of the people form great negative effects in minds.

There deceit, being with another man's woman, managing many women together in one's life, and adultery are encouraged. Family life, on the other hand, is constantly reflected as troubled, full of problems and impossible to carry on. And this leaves a very bad effect on women, men, the young and children. Because of this, young men and girls generally see marriage as troublesome and as a risk. The tendency of these young people to postpone marriage to the age of 30 and above is increasing.

Furthermore, the phenomenon of violence in the marriage programmes leaves very negative effects on individuals. Broadcasts of this kind, which are expressed from one side only and whose proof is not possible because the matter passed between two persons, also cause bad thoughts to form about the institution of the family.

k- The laws not being arranged in a manner suited to the Anatolian style of family

Some articles were later added to the Swiss civil code that was adopted at first, but these added articles too did not coincide with the belief, culture and custom of the people of Anatolia. Some arrangements have been made in this field within the frame of the laws of harmonisation with the European Union. However, we shall dwell here on the most important ones.

The family being left without a head:

By not appointing a head between the married woman and man, the family has been left without a head. Whereas the family is a partnership, and a partnership necessarily has a president. Even in the simplest companies there is an administrator as chairman of the board or as manager. Acting in this way in the family has increased conflict and dispute within the family.

Adultery being left free:

That adultery was removed from being a crime and became free has been a factor that increases divorces. Spouses who feel that they have been deceived have gone straight to the court.

Giving weight to one of the parties:

That the laws issued especially in recent periods are directed at the principle of positive discrimination for women upsets the balance between the parties. With these laws man and woman come face to face. At the point reached in Europe today, men are forming associations and civil society organisations to defend themselves and are struggling against these laws of positive discrimination. Unfortunately the direction things are taking in Turkey shows this as well.

l- Feminist Movements

Feminist movements took as their basis, first and foremost, the struggle between woman and man, and began the matter by declaring the man an enemy. Fundamentally the equality of woman and man was defended, but by going to excess on this subject the result was turned into enmity.

They acted with the claim "Men have not given women their rights.", and with these claims they have made the conflicts within the family more violent.

Claims of this kind found growth among Muslim women, and they too began to say:

"Muslim men do not give women their rights."

Both the former and the latter claim go no further than a supposition that has to be proved. Half of the world population of seven billion, and half of the Muslim population of two billion, are men. On this subject there has been neither a scientific study, nor statistics, nor a survey that encompasses the whole. These expressions have gone no further than being a statement with nothing beneath it.

2. The Particular and Internal Factors of Divorce

a- The feeling of "I", not being able to be "we"

The philosophy of "I exist in myself, I do not exist in you or in anyone else" is destroying the family.

The philosophy of "I exist in myself, I do not exist in you or in anyone else" is destroying the family. The man and the woman who marry need to be a whole, each as the half of one apple. Islam sees the woman as the garment of the man and the man as the garment of the woman, that is, as coverings that complete each other. And this means the spouses becoming whole, one body and one. The coming to the fore of the self, growing selfish and selfish attitudes affect the relation between the parties in a very bad way and cause quarrels and disputes.

b- The psychological and sociological factors that affect the individual

In relations within the family the psychology of woman and of man holds a very important place. If this psychology is not protected by the parties, the wholeness is spoiled and quarrels and noise begin to appear. And this speeds up the ruin of the family.

In our people's view of the family there are elements that carry great negativity. The negative talk that begins even before marrying and continues in the marriage too damages the wholeness of the family.

The understanding that looks on the son-in-law as "a stranger's son" and on the daughter-in-law as "a stranger's daughter" prevents seeing him or her as one's own and as a member of one's family. Or else the saying to the girl who is to be a bride, by her mother or her relatives:

"You are a stranger's daughter there. Whatever you do, it is in vain, they will not accept you." destroys the idea of the wholeness of the family in the minds of our girls. Parties who begin the marriage negatively at the very outset can also experience very great problems after marrying, within the frame of these understandings.

c- The woman's desire to establish superiority in the period when the family enters a bottleneck

People and families do not always have the same way of life. A family that was rich at one time becomes poor, and the poor one too can grow rich. Men too meet the same situations. They can have a way of life that is at times normal, at times bad, at times very good. This is the test of Allah among His servants.

When there is wealth the problems disappear or the wrongs are overlooked. But when there is poverty, hardship or debt, conflicts and quarrels begin in the family, and some women use this situation with the aim of establishing superiority over the man. The woman who brings up the hardship, the difficulty and the debts every day now falls to nagging and comes into a repellent position in the eyes of the man.

In fact the woman, while meaning to establish superiority, prepares the ruin in the family with her own hands. For this situation causes a great part of the men in Anatolia to look on the woman as the spouse who abandons him in his hard moments and leaves him alone and without support. And this damages the relation between the spouses and after a while brings separation with it.

d- The absence of equivalence/kafa'a between woman and man

In marriage the equivalence of woman and man is a very important factor.

In marriage the equivalence of woman and man is a very important factor. Islamic law gives very great importance to the question of equivalence. Indeed it permits spouses who have no equivalence to be separated by decision of the court and their contracts of marriage to be annulled.

But what comes first is that, before marrying, attention must be paid to the parties being equivalent, and this must be observed to a great extent.

Generally a race to prevail begins between spouses who are not equivalent to each other. Thus the spouses leave their essential duties, busy themselves with other matters and damage their marriage. Matters such as cultured and uncultured, rich and poor, beautiful and ugly, disabled and not disabled and the like pose a problem between families. And at times positions and situations that are not a problem are raised to the level of a problem, and many a time married life suffers ruin. However, when one looks at the section on "kafa'a/equivalence in marriage" of Islamic fiqh, it is possible to find broad explanations relating to this.

e- The phenomenon of living together without marriage

This understanding occurs mostly among the rich, in high society and in the world of music and film. This way of life is also encouraged by means of films and serials. Keeping a mistress or living together, when it becomes known by the spouse, mostly results in divorces.

Furthermore, the notion of living together with a woman like husband and wife, without taking on the burden of marriage, without being bound by any contract and without assuming any legal responsibility, is spreading more and more in the capitalist countries. This situation is also spreading in the Islamic countries where capitalism is dominant, and in other countries.

f- The woman's view of the man, and the man's view of the woman, being only as a sexual object

The contract of marriage/marriage is the sole contract that ensures the continuation of the human race by the lawful way. The family, the smallest unit of society, is formed by this contract. The spouses, by marrying, attain a warm home in the setting of a family, have children whom they will love and caress, and obtain a place in which they themselves will be sheltered and will carry on every kind of human activity. These needs are indispensable and necessary demands for everyone, woman and man.

When marriage is looked at only as a sexual object, when the woman's view of the man and the man's view of the woman is sexuality alone, one strays into the tendency to find this in different persons, and when the parties do not make each other happy in the sexual respect the searching begins. The fine things that the family setting brings with it are forgotten, and the matter comes down to sexuality and stays there. These searches naturally overflow outside the family home, and in the end the ruin of the family comes into question.

g- Seeing marriage as a burden and not taking risks

A disease that is spreading, especially among the young today, is also that marriage is seen as a burden and a trouble. In most young people the worry that they will not be able to carry the burden of marriage is also dominant. The most important factor that causes this is that the rates of unemployment in Turkey are very high and unemployment continues to be the most important social problem.

Another disease spreading among the young is the desire for a setting in which one will live one's freedom at ease without recognising any limit or bound. Because of this they see marriage as an institution that restricts freedom and is an obstacle to living as one wishes. And in the end they move away from marriage, indeed flee from it.

Some expressions that are spreading among women and men in society also support the idea of seeing marriage as a burden.

"Are you going to answer to a man for your every breath!"

"Are you going to listen to a woman's nagging every day!"

"Youth comes once, live your freedom!"

"Do not put your youth in shackles by marrying at an early age!" For example, this is said to our young people aged 25 or 26. Whereas the ages of 25 and 26 are counted a normal age for marriage even today. When one goes further back, these ages were assessed as a late age.

The formula of the nuclear family also increases these troubles. When everyone who marries opens a new home, heaps of expenses arise, and this frightens the young, in spite of the help of the families.

h- The negative effects on the individual of the divorces around him

i- Intolerance

j- The excessive love of families for their children

k- The man's desire to marry other women

l- Money, matter and position coming to the fore in individuals

This situation is a great social problem. When matter comes to the fore, the colour of relations changes. When matter becomes the standard of value and money too is made a god, everything is assessed in this manner. The relations of husband and wife, and the relations between the family and the relatives, are not taken up on the spiritual and social plane, and this affects the relation between the parties in a negative direction.

Everything is looked at with the eye of material interest, calm is sought in matter, and consequently family relations grow weak.

In general, although the causes that lead to the ending of marriage in Turkey can be very varied, the causes most often seen are these:29

  • Economic problems
  • The differences in the socio-cultural make-up of the spouses
  • Sexual problems
  • Breakdown of communication
  • The betrayal of one of the spouses
  • Violence within the family
  • Differences of taste
  • The spouses' neglect of each other
  • The excessive interference of the families
  • One or both of the spouses being open to the interference of the mother and father without observing justice
  • Not having children
  • Serious economic and class differences
  • The difference in age being great
  • Marriages made under family pressure
  • The sudden change of the personality and practice of life of one of the parties
  • Psychiatric problems/depression, panic attack, schizophrenia and so on
  • Addiction to drugs, alcohol and gambling

For this reason a man will leave his mother and his father and be joined to his wife, and the two will be one body.

b. Malachi 2:16

The LORD, the God of Israel, says, "I hate divorce."

c. Deuteronomy 24:1-4

1 If a man finds something unseemly in the woman he has married, and because of this is not pleased with her, writes a certificate of divorce and gives it to her and sends her out of his house,

2 and if the woman, after leaving the man's house, marries another,

3 and her second husband too is not pleased with her, writes a certificate of divorce and gives it and sends her out of his house, or if the second man dies,

4 her first husband, who divorced the woman, cannot marry her again. For the woman has been defiled. This is detestable in the eyes of the LORD. Do not lead into sin the land that the LORD your God will give you as a possession.

B. The New Testament

a. Matthew 19:3-12

3 Some Pharisees who came to Jesus asked Him this, with the aim of testing Him: "Is it in keeping with the Holy Law for a man to divorce his wife for any reason whatever?"

4-5 Jesus gave this answer: "Have you not read the Holy Scriptures? The Creator from the beginning 'created human beings male and female' and said: 'For this reason a man will leave his mother and his father and be joined to his wife, and the two will be one body.'

10 The disciples said to Jesus, "If this is the relation between a man and his wife, it is better not to marry at all!"

11 Jesus said to them, "Not everyone can accept this word, only those to whom God gives the strength can accept it."

12 For some are eunuchs from birth, some are made eunuchs by people, and some count themselves eunuchs for the sake of the Kingdom of Heaven. Let whoever can accept this accept it!

28 But I say to you that every man who looks at a woman with lust has committed adultery with that woman in his heart.

29 If your right eye causes you to sin, pluck it out and throw it away. For it is better that one member of your body be lost than that your whole body be thrown into hell.

30 If your right hand causes you to sin, cut it off and throw it away. For it is better that one member of your body be lost than that your whole body go to hell.

31 It has been said, 'Whoever divorces his wife, let him give her a certificate of divorce.'

32 But I say to you that whoever divorces his wife for a reason other than fornication drives her to adultery. And whoever marries a divorced woman commits adultery.

7-8 For this reason a man will leave his mother and his father and be joined to his wife, and the two will be one body. So that they are no longer two, but one body.

9 Therefore what God has joined together, let no human being separate."

11 Jesus said to them, "Whoever divorces his wife and marries another commits adultery against his wife."

Whoever divorces his wife and marries another commits adultery. And whoever marries a woman divorced from her husband commits adultery.

11 If she separates, let her not marry, or let her be reconciled with her husband. And let the husband not divorce his wife.

f. Letter to the Romans

1 Do you not know, brothers, for I speak to those who know the Holy Law, that the Law rules over a person only as long as he lives?

2 For example, a married woman is bound by the law to her husband as long as her husband lives; if her husband dies, she is free from the law that binds her to her husband.

3 Accordingly, if the woman enters into a relation with another man while her husband is alive, she is counted as having committed adultery. But if her husband dies, the woman is free from the law. So that, if she marries another man, she does not commit adultery.
