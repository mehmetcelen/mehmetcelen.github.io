Halal Earnings

About 6 min read

Translated from the Turkish original.Read the original

Halal earning is one of the most basic characteristic features of Islamic civilisation. This civilisation rests, in all its dealings and practices, on what is lawful (halal) and permitted. The lawful is the rule, and there is no passage to the forbidden (haram). The lawful is encouraged and made attractive; people are warned away from the forbidden. What is lawful falls within the scope of worship, while the forbidden is punished. To turn to the lawful is a reward, and to incline to the forbidden is a sin.

On this subject many noble verses have come in the Noble Qur'an, the last book and divine message. Almighty Allah has addressed, on this matter, both the prophets whom He sent to bring humanity into the light and the believers, and has said:

"O messengers! Eat of the good things and do righteous deeds."1

"O you who believe! Eat of the good things that We have provided for you."2

What is clean, pleasant, good (tayyib) and fine has been commanded; what is foul, ugly, bad and filthy has been forbidden. In this connection the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) also said:

"O people! Allah is good and accepts nothing but what is good. What Allah has commanded the believers is the same as what He commanded the messengers." Then he recites the verses we have mentioned above and continues as follows:

He spoke of a traveller who makes a long journey, dishevelled and covered in dust, and who raises his hands to the sky and prays: "O my Lord! O my Lord!", and he said:

"What this traveller eats is forbidden, what he drinks is forbidden, what he wears is forbidden, and he is nourished with the forbidden. How can the prayer of such a person be answered?"3

In many other noble hadiths too this subject is treated at length. Let us mention a few of them here:

"The most excellent deed is halal earning."4

"To seek the halal is jihad."5

"To learn what the halal is and to strive to earn it is an obligation on every Muslim."6

As is stated here too, to seek the lawful, to be nourished by what is lawful, to eat what is lawful, to drink what is lawful, to dress in what is lawful, to be housed by lawful means and to strive to earn it is an obligatory act of worship and jihad in the way of Allah.

"What a person earns by the work of his hand and by honest trade is the purest and the most excellent earning."7

In another hadith it is said:

"No one has ever eaten better food than what comes from the work of his own hand. David (peace be upon him), the prophet of Allah, used to eat from the work of his hand."8

What a person earns by the sweat of his brow and the work of his hand is one of the most lawful of blessings. In trade too, transactions carried out honestly, without deceiving, without cheating one's customer, without lying, without swearing false oaths and without giving short measure or weight are the most acceptable ones.

The value and importance of halal earning

Halal earning has a very important place in the life of a Muslim. To seek the lawful, to set out on the road for it, to run to it and to turn to it alone is an obligatory act of worship, jihad and the most excellent of deeds. The road to Paradise too is paved with lawful things.

To move away from the lawful, to turn to the forbidden and to be closely involved with the forbidden is a sin and the most important factor that takes a person to Hell. The first sin in the world was committed by turning to the forbidden through eating the forbidden fruit, and it caused the human being to be driven out of Paradise. Almighty Allah, who says "We have created means of livelihood for you on the earth",9 has also said:

"We have placed everything on the earth, in the heavens and between the two at the service of the human being", stating plainly that He has made all beings ready for the human being.

Thus the earth, the heavens, the stars in the sky, the planets, the galaxies, air, water, the sun, plants, animals and the whole of the universe have been placed at the service of humanity. In accordance with the very important rule in fiqh, "The basic principle regarding things is permissibility", if there is no definite and firm ruling that a thing or a being is forbidden, that thing is lawful. Seen from this point of view, the things that are forbidden are very few and limited, while the lawful things are very many. Almost everything our eye can take in is within the scope of the lawful.

The great majority of the living creatures in the sea are lawful. Indeed, according to the great mujtahid Imam Malik (may Allah have mercy on him), the founder of the Maliki school, all the animals found in the sea are lawful.

All permitted animals hunted in accordance with the rule are lawful.

Cattle, sheep and goats that chew the cud are lawful.

Among flying animals, those other than the birds of prey with talons are lawful.

The great majority of land animals that eat with their beaks are lawful.

All the plants, vegetables and fruits on the earth are lawful, except those that are harmful.

All the fresh waters on the earth are lawful.

It is lawful to use the water of the sea.

Earth, water, fire and air, the four elements of life, are lawful things offered for the benefit of humanity.

It is lawful for humanity to use, within legitimate measures, the minerals and useful substances under the ground.

We could multiply still further the lawful things we have listed above. For this reason our surroundings are filled to the brim with lawful things. Because of the abundance of the lawful, there is no setting in which we would have need of the forbidden.

There are in the world enough lawful foods and nutrients to feed existing humankind many times over. When one thinks about it in the real sense, there is no logic either in reaching out to the forbidden and eating what is forbidden. The person who uses his mind keeps away from the forbidden altogether and never inclines in that direction.

Lawful things sufficient for all, Muslims, Christians, Jews, Buddhists, atheists, polytheists and non-Muslims, have been offered to humanity by Allah. The duty that falls to humanity is to give thanks to its exalted Creator in return for all these blessings.
