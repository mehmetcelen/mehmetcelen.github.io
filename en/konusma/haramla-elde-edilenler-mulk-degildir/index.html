What Is Obtained through the Forbidden Is Not Property

About 28 min read

Translated from the Turkish original.Read the original

  2. Property that is forbidden
  3. The ruling on forbidden property

  5. How is forbidden property cleansed?

Before looking at the question that what is obtained through the forbidden (haram) is not property, let us try to explain the general principles concerning the forbidden and the things that are obtained by forbidden means.

General principles concerning the forbidden

The authority to make a thing forbidden belongs in the first degree to Allah. Since the owner of the religion is Allah, the authority to determine the forbidden and the lawful is His too. The Prophet gives a ruling of prohibition within the framework of the authority that Allah has given him. The mujtahids too may bring rulings of prohibition in line with the authority given to them by Allah and His Messenger, according to established principles and in accordance with the spirit of Islam.

Things that are a means to the forbidden and lead to the forbidden are also forbidden. If what is done results in causing the forbidden to be committed, it too comes under the ruling of the forbidden. For example, knowingly selling grapes, which are lawful in themselves, to a wine factory.

The firmly established prohibitions are forbidden in every place and time. The firmly established (muhkam) prohibitions are the prohibitions that Allah has laid down in His Book and His Messenger in his sound and noble Sunna, such as drinking alcohol, committing adultery, killing a person unjustly, gambling and the like.

One must avoid matters that carry a doubt of being forbidden. In transactions involving interest in particular, if there is a doubt of the forbidden, it is treated as forbidden. Even if there are no complete and definite rulings on its being forbidden, one must avoid situations that are close to the forbidden and carry a doubt of it.

Cases of necessity make the forbidden permissible. This holds for cases without which human life itself cannot continue. For example, a person who has come to the point of death from hunger eating carrion (the flesh of a dead animal), or a person who is about to die of thirst drinking wine.

The forbidden does not change according to place and time. A thing that is forbidden in every place and time is still under the ruling of the forbidden. In general terms, in non-Muslim countries the forbidden things are not made subject to the ruling of the lawful.

Prohibitions and bans reached by ijtihad are each mujtahid's own ruling and in religion bind only himself. Other Muslims may learn the proofs for it and follow this ruling, and then act according to the view of that mujtahid.

The bans that bind society are the decisions taken, as a result of consultation (shura), by a Muslim government that applies Islamic law in every field or by an assembly of scholars. The consultative assembly may take certain decisions and lay down bans in questions that concern society. In an assembly made up of scholars of Islam, certain prohibitive decisions are taken, unanimously or by majority, for the adherents of the religion of Islam, and these decisions are binding on the Muslim society in that region.

Property that is forbidden

We shall speak in turn of the buying and selling, acquiring of property, leasing, transactions and conduct that are forbidden according to Islamic law.

Goods, cash and jewellery obtained by theft are forbidden.

Theft is to take a thing that is under protection and does not belong to one.

Islamic law has made theft forbidden and has laid down that the hadd punishment be applied to those who steal, as a sanction, so that this crime may not spread in society and people may not be drawn to it.

However, Islamic law has tied the application of this hadd punishment to the condition that social security and every kind of insurance be provided and that no one be left without work, hungry or thirsty in society. In societies where unemployment prevails and social security is not provided, this hadd punishment is not applied. For unemployment means hunger and heads of families being reduced to such helplessness that they cannot take bread home.

Indeed 'Umar (may Allah be pleased with him), the caliph of the Muslims and second head of state after the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), rebuked the master who had caused his slave to steal by leaving him hungry, and stated sternly that if this happened a second time he would apply the hadd punishment not to the slave but to the master. In the end he did not apply the hadd punishment to this slave who had stolen. Muslim rulers did not apply the hadd punishment to those who stole in times of famine either. The first to apply this was again 'Umar (may Allah be pleased with him).

It is forbidden to obtain money and valuables by snatching, highway robbery and pickpocketing.

These crimes take place by snatching by force bags that do not belong to one, by filching the money in someone else's pocket or by pilfering goods from a shop.

All of these acts have been made forbidden because in them a person takes a thing that does not belong to him, its price is not paid and it is obtained unjustly.

It is forbidden to obtain the goods of orphans and of the weak by usurping them or by seizing their real estate by force.

Almighty Allah has forbidden even approaching the property of orphans unjustly. Orphans are people without strength, and in the same way approaching the goods of weak people who have no power and passing them unjustly into one's own ownership has been forbidden.

"Do not approach the property of the orphan, except in the best manner, until he reaches maturity."

"Do not consume the property of the orphan unjustly."

It is permissible for the guardians and trustees of orphans and weak persons to take from the wards' own property the amounts spent on their food, drink, clothing, shelter, education and teaching and their other expenses. For they will have used and disposed of the property belonging to the orphans and the weak for the orphans and the weak themselves. For that reason the goods and cash taken in return for the amount spent are not forbidden.

Here too what is fundamental is not to go to waste and luxury, not to be miserly either, and to use the authority one has been given in a measured and balanced way. To be miserly where spending is needed and to suppose that one is protecting the orphan's property by not spending money is not thrift. Indeed this may bring the guardian or trustee into sin, for he is considered not to have duly fulfilled his responsibilities towards the persons entrusted to him. Thrift means spending in a measured way in the places where spending is needed.

Cash and real estate obtained by dealing in interest and usury are forbidden.

It makes no difference whether dealing in interest is done by institutions or by an individual. Islamic law has forbidden giving cash, gold, foreign currency, foodstuffs or goods as a loan to those in difficulty or in need and then taking an excess over the amount given.

However, our scholars of fiqh have put forward two views on the difference for deferred payment that arises in situations where inflation prevails. The different interpretations have arisen from the fact that the relation between inflation and money changes the way real values are looked at. For this reason, in these situations some of the scholars have held that the difference for deferred payment is permissible, and others that it is not.

We too are of the opinion that in such situations the practice of a difference for deferred payment is permissible, on condition that it be measured.

Everything obtained by selling with short weight and with deficient and deceptive units of measure is forbidden.

In particular, to sell foodstuffs, vegetables and fruit, and building materials that can be weighed, by giving short weight or by deliberately misleading the customer is forbidden and a sin.

To give short measure in fabrics, fields, building plots, buildings, flats and shops and everything that can be measured by the metre, and to give misleading information in this matter, is forbidden.

The verse says: "Woe to those who give short measure and weight!"

Cash, movables and goods obtained by engaging in organised crime and by the use of force and coercion in order to obtain goods unjustly are forbidden.

To form armed gangs and to enter into mafia organisation in order to obtain unjust earnings, goods and property is forbidden. Structures of this kind are contrary to the spirit of Islam.

To seize and usurp people's goods by oppressing them and doing them injustice is forbidden.

Cash, movables and property obtained by selling the flesh of dead animals (mayta, carrion) of the kinds that are lawful to eat, and the animal slaughtered in the name of other than Allah, are forbidden. Included in this are also the flesh of animals that have died by being strangled, by being beaten, by being gored, by falling from a height or by being torn by wild beasts. These too come under the ruling of carrion.

It is also forbidden to make an animal carrion by slaughtering it without observing the Islamic methods of slaughter, so bringing it under the ruling of mayta, and then to sell its flesh. Eating the flesh of the dead bodies of animals that are lawful to eat has been made forbidden to Muslims. "Allah has forbidden carrion…"

It is also forbidden to sell or to buy the carrion of small livestock such as sheep and goats, of large livestock such as cows, oxen and camels, and of animals such as hens, turkeys and cocks, and to help in buying and selling them.

Cash, movables and property obtained by selling pork and its products are forbidden.

Almighty Allah has said on these matters: "He has forbidden you only carrion, blood, the flesh of swine and that over which any name other than Allah's has been invoked. But whoever is driven by necessity, neither desiring it nor exceeding the limit, there is no sin upon him. Surely Allah is Most Forgiving, Most Merciful." (al-Baqara, 2:173)

Cash, movables and property obtained as a result of giving news of the unseen by fortune-telling, soothsaying, dealing with jinn and acting as a medium are forbidden.

To deceive people with coffee-cup reading, tarot, divining arrows and by giving news of the future (the unseen) is forbidden. It causes people to move away from the truth and keeps them from looking at life in its real aspect. Islam has forbidden superstitious beliefs of this kind and has called people to avoid them strictly.

"Only Allah knows the unseen." No one other than Allah can know the unseen. Those who claim to know it are the very liars themselves.

Cash and property obtained by selling and trading in intoxicating substances (wine, rakı, cognac, champagne, vodka, gin, tonic, beer and so on) and narcotics (opium, morphine, codeine, heroin, cocaine, hashish, mescaline, ecstasy, pills, MDA, MDE, LSD and so on) are forbidden.

These substances, whether in liquid form or in the form of powder and solids, destroy a person's nervous system and also cause many illnesses. These substances, which above all destroy the faculty of using the mind, have been strictly prohibited and made forbidden in the religion of Islam. These substances, which numb and intoxicate a person, at the same time trample human dignity underfoot and strip a person of his humanity.

It is also forbidden to sell, to buy and to act as a go-between for the volatile solvents (glues, thinner and so on) that are used in building and in certain fields, for the purpose of drugging people and as narcotics.

Those who take these narcotics become, in time, addicted to the substance and dependent both psychologically and physically. The fact that these substances give pleasure increases people's dependence still further. As well as inviting many illnesses, they also give rise to many bad traits, such as withdrawal from social life and from life itself, loss of human qualities, brutalisation, aggressiveness and indifference to the world and to events.

In verse 90 of Sura al-Ma'ida it is said: "O you who believe! Khamr, gambling, stone altars and divining arrows are but an abomination of Satan's work. So avoid them, that you may prosper."

The expression "khamr" in this noble verse means "wine", and it also means "everything that veils the mind". The meaning "that which suspends or destroys the function of the mind" is the stronger definition.

Money and property obtained from prostitution (the white slave trade), from acting as a go-between in the trade of prostitution and from using women and men in evil ways are forbidden.

In Islamic law it has been forbidden to enslave women, to create a sector of sex slavery and to trade through them and make money. This field, which in our day has become a commercial sector, especially in the capitalist countries, and which is given the name of the entertainment sector, darkens the lives of thousands of women, boys and girls, steals their future and poisons the life of this world for them.

Generally women and children are abducted by force and sold to these establishments that trade in prostitution. The women and children held in these places are made to sign promissory notes for large sums and are thus placed under a great debt, and these people, who are forced to pay off their debt for a lifetime, are enslaved. In this way free human beings are enslaved and a crime against humanity is committed.

Money and property obtained from gambling, gambling machines and games of chance (the national lottery, betting, lotto, football pools, horse-race betting, scratch cards, tarot, throwing dice, cash prize draws and so on) are forbidden.

In verse 90 of Sura al-Ma'ida it is said: "O you who believe! Khamr, gambling, stone altars and divining arrows are but an abomination of Satan's work. So avoid them, that you may prosper."

The Arab polytheists used to carry out with divining arrows, in a different form, the operations that we today call games of chance. With this noble verse, moreover, every kind of gambling, which ruins homes, breaks up families and prevents people from fulfilling their responsibilities, has been forbidden.

Cash and property obtained by carrying out contract killings and beatings oneself, by having them carried out or by acting as a go-between are forbidden.

It is forbidden to hire gunmen in order to kill someone, to have him killed, to harm him or to wound him so as to acquire money and property unjustly, and in this way to obtain wealth. Those who act as go-betweens in injustices of this kind and those who knowingly give support are also counted as partners in the same crime, and the money they obtain in this way comes under the ruling of the forbidden.

Those who act as hired gunmen and suppose that they are receiving the return for their labour obtain nothing but the forbidden either. For to kill people, to cause them loss and to wound them in return for money is a forbidden act prohibited by religion. For our religion proceeds from the thought: "Whoever kills a human being unjustly has killed all of humanity"; it has regarded this act as very ugly and has rejected it strongly.

Cash and property obtained by taking bribes and by helping and acting as a go-between in this are forbidden.

Bribery is conspicuous especially among public officials and in large companies and firms. Transactions of this kind are seen from the lowest to the highest among civil servants, directors general, managers, persons in authority and workers.

All civil servants and workers receive a salary from their own institutions in return for the work they do and for their labour. They must carry out, without taking anything in return from the citizen, the business that is brought to them by the citizen and that is normally their own work. For any civil servant or worker to take anything in return from the citizen while doing what is normally his own work is an unjust gain and is forbidden.

Cash and property obtained by not giving the people one employs the return for their labour and by consuming their due are forbidden.

It is not permissible, when one's income is good and one's means are sufficient, to pay the workers or staff one employs a low salary, not to pay for overtime, not to give their bonuses and compensation, to make them work much and pay them little, and not to pay an amount equal to their labour.

Not to give to those entitled, when there is no excuse whatever, the rights relating to working hours, bonuses, compensation and additional payments set out in contracts is a usurpation of rights. All cash and goods obtained in this way come under the ruling of the forbidden. For this employer has disposed of property that does not belong to him.

Although on the surface it looks as if the commissions taken in tenders are paid by the company, in fact it is society that pays this bill.

The cash and property corresponding to the amount of 10%, 15% or 20% demanded, by hidden or open ways, as a "tender commission" from those who win the tenders held in certain public bodies and municipalities or in large companies are forbidden.

The demand for a tender commission, which has become almost normal in our country, has caused thousands of consumers of the forbidden to come into being. Although on the surface it looks as if the commissions taken in tenders are paid by the company, in fact it is society that pays this bill. For the excess paid there is collected by the company, and more, from the public budget or from the municipal budget.

Both the public budget and the municipal budget are the budget of society and the property of society. The officials there are only trustees. The officials who take these tender commissions of 10%, 15% or 20% are both consuming the forbidden by taking property that does not belong to them and betraying the trust. Some shameless officials even carry out these evil deeds of theirs while saying again and again: "In this there is the due of the orphan whose down has not yet grown." But with this they deceive only themselves, and they enter into great sins by feeding these forbidden goods and cash only to their own families and relatives. Meanwhile, in the sharing out of the forbidden property they see no one but themselves or those who help them.

Yet the officials who conduct the tender receive a salary in return for the work they do and come to enjoy certain social rights. For them to receive, in addition, cheques, cash and real estate for these tasks, which they carry out as their own duty, is an unjust gain and is not their right. By doing so they usurp the property of society unjustly, like the forty thieves.

Cash and property obtained by black marketeering are forbidden.

Black marketeering appears in particular in foodstuffs that the people need very much and in basic necessities. It is the secret sale of a commodity at very high prices in years of famine, in periods when production is low, or demand is high and supply is not sufficient, or the commodity is very scarce.

As a result of some sharp and opportunist tradesmen and merchants buying up the commodity and building up stocks, great rises are seen in prices. Thus a commodity worth ten lira begins to be sold at exorbitant prices such as twenty, thirty or forty lira. It is in this period that the hoarders who caused the prices to rise take their goods out of stock, put them on the market and begin to sell them at an exorbitant price. In this way they obtain unjust gain on the back of society. Goods and property obtained in this manner come under the ruling of the forbidden.

It is forbidden not to give the zakat and to keep in hand, use and consume the amount that ought to be given.

It is forbidden for the wealthy Muslim to keep in his hand, by not giving it, the zakat that Allah has made obligatory on him and that in religion is the due not of himself but of the poor.

The Muslim view and way of thinking on this subject is as follows. For example, if a Muslim has a hundred gold pieces, that Muslim does not think that he is the owner of the whole of the hundred gold pieces. He accepts that 97.5 gold pieces are his own property and that 2.5 gold pieces are the property of the poor (zakat). Thus he does not lay claim to the whole of the hundred gold pieces. He willingly gives the portion of 2.5% to the eight classes that Allah has stated in His Book, first among them the poor.

Sales made by deceiving the customer, by lying and by concealing the defect of the goods are forbidden. The cash and property obtained in this way are also forbidden.

After emigrating to Medina, the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) set up a Muslim market as an alternative to the polytheists. One day he went round this market and carried out an inspection. In one such inspection he plunged his hand into a sack, observed that the top of the produce was dry and the bottom wet, and said:

"Whoever deceives us is not one of us."

Yes, to deceive people and to lie, in whatever field it may be, has been made forbidden to Muslims. Therefore trading by deceit has also been made forbidden.

It is forbidden to engage in the organ mafia and to obtain income in this way.

In particular, to abduct poor people or to deceive them, take their organs in return for small sums and sell them to the rich for large sums of money is forbidden. To occupy oneself with organised dealings of this kind is in itself forbidden. For the human body is inviolable and has been entrusted to the person. The sale of any organ of it is never permissible. Nor does any person have the right to say of his own body: this is my body, I do as I wish.

It is forbidden, when one is rich and well off, not to pay one's debt and to consume that money, or to register the real estate in one's own name.

Those who do not pay the debts they have taken from people although they have the means to pay, and who consume them, consume nothing but the forbidden. The same does not hold for those who do not have the means. As the verse of the Qur'an commands, those who do not have the means must be given time until they do. Here those who are able and those who are not are not weighed in the same scale. Those who fall into sin are those who do not pay their debts although they have the means.

The ruling on forbidden property

For the common sense of the people is very important in looking at questions and shows us the way in many matters.

Before stating the ruling on forbidden property and its place in religion, let us try to see how our Muslim people look at the forbidden. For the common sense of the people is very important in looking at questions and shows us the way in many matters.

There are many proverbs and sayings about the forbidden that pass from mouth to mouth. The great majority of them are a concise expression either of verses or of noble hadiths. For example:

"The forbidden has no foundation, no building."

This proverb expresses that what is obtained through the forbidden is not property. It states that goods that come through the forbidden are a rotten foundation and a building doomed to collapse quickly. People of this type are seen a great deal in society too. While in need of a loaf of bread they suddenly become rich with forbidden money, and before a few years have passed all the wealth in the hands of some of these same persons has vanished.

"There is no cure in the forbidden."

In saying this it is stated that money obtained by forbidden means cannot be a source of healing for a person. It is also meant that it is not possible for foods and drinks that are forbidden to be a source of healing. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said in a sound hadith:

"Almighty Allah has not created a cure for you in the things that are forbidden to you."

The meaning intended here in saying "the forbidden has no cure" is to occupy oneself directly with the forbidden in ordinary matters and without using the lawful. It is this that is prohibited. Otherwise, if the use of forbidden substances in the treatment of illnesses is a necessity, then on account of necessity they come under the ruling of the permissible and become allowed.

"The prayer of one who consumes the forbidden is not accepted."

This saying is the concise wording and expression of the following hadith of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), which explains that the supplication of a person whose food, drink and clothing are forbidden will not be accepted:

"O people! Allah is good and accepts nothing but what is good. What Allah has commanded the believers is the same as what He commanded the messengers." Then he recites the verses we have mentioned above and continues as follows:

He spoke of a traveller who makes a long journey, dishevelled and covered in dust, and who raises his hands to the sky and prays: "O my Lord! O my Lord!", and he said:

"What this traveller eats is forbidden, what he drinks is forbidden, what he wears is forbidden, and he is nourished with the forbidden. How can the prayer of such a person be answered?"

In our day our people use the expression "haramzâde" for those who consume the forbidden constantly and in quantity. In fact the expression haramzâde is a word that has long been used for "illegitimate children". But the consuming of the forbidden has become so widespread in our society that people have begun to give this name to such persons. In a story told from of old, the expression "the forty thieves" (kırk haramîler) is used for the band that waylaid people and lived by usurping their goods. Haramî means one who consumes the forbidden, who is occupied with the forbidden and who lives day and night with the forbidden. The following sayings are also often used:

"The bread of the haramzâde is not to be eaten."

"He who consumes the forbidden will never prosper."

Our people say these sayings in order to show their great reaction against those who consume the forbidden. Starting from the fact that the forbidden has no foundation, it is expressed very concisely that such people cannot prosper and cannot reach salvation. That the food of the haramzâde is not eaten is in order to prevent their forbidden morsels from passing down the throats of clean people. The forbidden morsel darkens the body, plunges a person into sin and leads him to rebellion. For a human being is shaped by what he eats and drinks. In short, the pithy saying "What is needed for the mosque is forbidden to the tavern" declares everything very plainly.

The religious rulings on forbidden property are as follows

Islamic law has not counted goods obtained by forbidden means as property. It has never accepted that goods obtained unjustly are a cause for the right of ownership to be established. For it has not established in religion that these goods belong to that person. For it to be established in religion, the transaction must be carried out by lawful and legitimate means.

According to the rules of Islamic law, contracts that are void provide no benefit to the party and grant him no right of ownership.

For example, the act of theft does not constitute a cause for the establishment of ownership. Usurpation, seizing goods by force and every kind of unjust acquisition attach no positive result for the doers of these acts, and the benefit that was sought is not recognised.

Forbidden goods, and contracts relating to interest or to the sale of mayta (carrion), are contracts that are wholly void and invalid. According to the rules of Islamic law, contracts that are void provide no benefit to the party and grant him no right of ownership. Through contracts of this kind the result that is attached to legitimate and valid contracts is not attached. Legitimate and lawful transactions, on the other hand, grant the person the right of ownership and of use.

In conclusion, since these forbidden acts are held to be void and invalid, both these contracts and every kind of goods obtained unjustly grant no right of ownership to the doer of the act. Since there is no right of ownership, the person does not have the authority to dispose of that property or the right to benefit from it either.

Charity, alms (sadaqa), spending in the way of Allah and good works are not done with forbidden goods. Nor are acts of worship such as the sacrifice and the pilgrimage (hajj) performed with forbidden goods. Even if they are performed, these acts of worship are not accepted in the sight of Allah. For it is a principle that all these acts of worship be performed from lawful earnings.

It will be fitting to explain the following example here. A Muslim who goes on the pilgrimage with forbidden money is not held to have fulfilled the worship of hajj. According to what is reported in a hadith narrated among the sound hadiths, "the person who performs the worship of hajj in keeping with its conditions and obligations has all his sins forgiven and becomes like a newborn human being." But the person who goes on the pilgrimage with forbidden money has not gone with lawful money, which is the most important condition of the hajj, and has failed from the outset to fulfil the conditions of the hajj. Since the money spent was not lawful, the worship of hajj has been void and invalid. For that reason the saying "all his sins are forgiven" has not held for him. Indeed, because he did not keep to its conditions and went on the pilgrimage with forbidden money, instead of earning reward he has become sinful as well. For he has set at nought the conditions and principles that must be met for an act of worship. The most important thing for this person to do is to cleanse his money by casting out what is forbidden in it and to make it lawful.

How is forbidden property cleansed?

When a Muslim commits a sin, the first thing he must do is to abandon that sin at once. To obtain goods by forbidden means is also a sin and calls for repentance. What a sincere repentance requires is first of all to give up the sin committed, to resolve not to do it again and to feel remorse for having done it.

But if there is a right of another person in this sin, he must also return this right to its owners. When the right is not returned to its owners, this repentance is not sincere and is not accepted in the sight of Allah, for the conditions of repentance have not been fulfilled.

Of goods and money obtained through interest, all the rest apart from the capital must be returned to the persons from whom the interest was taken. The capital is the person's own property; the interest beyond it is forbidden property.

However, interest received from interest-based institutions should not be left again to the interest-based institution. It is not right to support them by giving this interest to the interest-based institution. The interest money received from interest-based institutions should be given to the poor and needy in society.

An employer who has usurped the money of workers must, in order to cleanse the forbidden from his property, return even a single penny that was usurped to the workers entitled to it, one by one. If any worker has died, he must give that money to his heirs, trustee or guardian.

Forbidden money and property obtained from lotteries, football pools, lotto and games of chance must be given to the poor and needy in society, since its owners are not known and it is not possible to identify them. Only in this way is a person's capital cleansed of the forbidden.

Cash, jewellery and property obtained by theft, usurpation and force must first of all be returned to their real owners. If the real owner has died, then this property must be given to the heirs, trustee and guardian of the deceased. If the real owners of the cash obtained in this way are not known and there is an Islamic state there, this property is entered as revenue in the budget of the Islamic state. If there is no Islamic state in that region, then this money and property obtained by forbidden means must be given to the poor and needy in society.

To conclude, forbidden property provides a person with no right, possession, ownership or benefit; it is not a legitimate earning.

Our exalted Lord! Keep us, our selves and our descendants far from forbidden property!

Our Lord! Let our food, our drink and our clothing be from lawful earnings! Keep us together with the lawful and make us its constant companions!

O Allah! We seek refuge in You from eating a forbidden morsel! Keep us far from all that is forbidden!
