An Interview on Writing with the Researcher and Author Dr. Mehmet Çelen

I conducted this interview on 2 April 2016 in Fatih, Istanbul, at the home of the researcher and author Mehmet ÇELEN. Our author received us as his guests; during the visit I asked my questions and we also had our photograph taken together.

1- Could you tell us briefly about yourself?

I was born in Malatya in 1961; I am a researcher and a writer. I finished primary school, middle school and high school in Malatya. I completed university at Ondokuz Mayıs University and did my master's degree in the field of Islamic law at Istanbul University. I also did a master's degree in the department of "Political History and International Relations" at the Institute of Middle East Studies of Marmara University. I did my doctorate in the field of Islamic law and comparative law in Beirut, the capital of the Republic of Lebanon. At present I am continuing my work as an independent researcher at the Centre for Islamic Studies in Üsküdar.

2- Why did you choose the profession of writing and not some other work?

Since I was small I have loved reading, writing and listening. To obtain knowledge, reading and writing alone are not enough; listening is important too. One has to know how to listen as well. And because it develops a person culturally and intellectually, I have always been interested in this. I wrote my first book at the age of 21. That work was on morals. Its name was "Good Character and Ugly Conduct".

3- What are the points you pay most attention to in the profession of writing?

We take care that the works we write are academic and scholarly, documented and reasoned. We do not write about matters that are baseless and without proof, and we take care not to form sentences that would create suspicion and a false impression in people. We strive for the sentences we form and the words we choose to be useful both for people and for society. We prefer to use not a language that divides, excludes, belittles and insults, but expressions that bring together, embrace and include.

4- What is the force that drives you to write?

Since I also have the identity of a researcher, the more we research and reach new sources of knowledge, the more we run on to yet another new thing. It is like running to a new world and to new things. This gives a person eagerness and excitement. The excitement of new knowledge, or of producing real knowledge, becomes a person's triggering and driving force. As someone who works in a scholarly way, the more we learn, the more we see that the things we do not know are greater in number. So, in order to make good use of this short life of ours, we move into action all the more and try to strive to see how much more we can learn. And when we share what we have learned with society, works come into being.

5- Did you receive any special training for the profession of writing? What was it?

I did not receive training in the form of a writing workshop as there is today. In any case, in our time, in the 1970s and 1980s, there were no such courses. But I can say that I received a certain training. In those days I was very successful in my literature and composition classes in middle school, high school and university. I had a great interest in literature. In addition there was the Malatya Thought Club, which, with my teachers taking part, concerned itself with speaking, with writing and with what to pay attention to when writing. I attended it from middle school until I finished university. There we were taught the art of speaking well, and so we were learning both to speak and to write. When we spoke wrongly we were warned at once, and we understood our mistake. Even when we pronounced one word wrongly it was corrected. In the end we became more exact and more careful. For my own part, I have tried to use this quality gained in speaking in just the same way in writing.

6- What are the works you have written?

Up to today 22 works of mine have been published. Twelve of them are original works and ten are translations. Nine of the translations are from Arabic into Turkish and one is from English into Turkish. My doctoral thesis, however, has not been published. That too, God willing, I am thinking of publishing in Arabic in Beirut. The fields of knowledge in which I have published are law, politics, education and language. My own field in particular is Islamic law. But law and politics are present in our past works of fiqh. There you find politics and economics as well as law. Meanwhile, since I have also worked as an educator, I have written on education too.

Since I have taught languages, I have produced work on language, relating to the Arabic and English languages.

7- What was your aim in your latest work, entitled "The Guiding Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him)"?

To write the life of our master the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) was a privilege for me. Since he is the last prophet, and since his life can be lived and taken as an example, he has an important place in our lives. We tried to express this in writing and with the pen; if we have been able to succeed, I shall say how happy that is.

8- Finally, what advice would you give to us young people?

I would like to advise my young friends as follows. If you look at it truly, I too am young, but for the young people who are younger than I am I would like to say this:

Let them first think about themselves. They need to try to read and to be cultured. As human beings they need to think about and look into the answers to questions such as "What am I?", "Why do I exist?", "What shall I become?" and "What ought I to become?" Or they need to seek and find the answer to questions such as "Why were we brought into existence in the world?" and "What will our end be?" In daily life too I would particularly like them to read; I would advise them to be at least university graduates and, if they have the means, to do a master's degree and a doctorate.

Their taking an interest in this reading and research will make them knowledgeable and cultured. I advise them in particular not to busy themselves with needless things on social media. For these days we see that the pollution of information in that field is very great. That is, I advise a young person not to treat his life as worthless by busying himself with things that do not concern him, that are needless, empty and useless. Truly life is meaningful and very valuable. We too, as human beings, have very important qualities in the world of beings. By making use of these qualities of ours, by using well a blessing such as the mind, by thinking, by reasoning and by looking into the inner face of events, one must arrive at what is right. One must also look into what is right and ask about it, and read a great deal. In our day reading has become easier; it has now moved onto tablets. Of course, from the point of view of the eyes, reading from the book itself has its healthy side. Because it is a screen, the tablet has qualities that constantly tire the eyes. That is why in the past the Ottoman scholars and students are said to have read books on yellow paper. The printing too is said to have been done on yellow or straw-coloured paper. For since reading went on for hours, they discovered this so as not to tire the eyes. In short, young people have the abilities to build their own future, but they have to discover this themselves and take on the responsibility for it. I wish that they may determine the course of life themselves and carry on their lives in a fine way.

I would also like to congratulate your esteemed teacher, who brings students together with our writers, thinkers and intellectuals. And I thank you for developing yourself by taking on such a fine assignment. I wish you success in your future life; may good days be yours.

I thank you too.
